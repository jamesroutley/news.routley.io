Apps are undergoing a phase transition

Apps are changing in an irreversible way. And all cause you can add a little thing to the bottom right.

Patrick Collison is more popular than me, so I'll let his words shine.

Apps are discrete right now. My phone's got loads of them. Christ knows it's too many. And I've gotten used to/by them. I switch between and my fingers are pretty fast at it now. Sometimes they twitch in its absence.

But I hate this. It sucks, going from Chrome to Plaid to Chase back to Plaid back to Chrome. I don't want to think about what the name of that app is, or how exactly it's different from a bunch of others. I wanna my hands off that phone. Maybe holding a girl's. Why can't the phone just work? Why do I have to suffer?

But AI changes it, like so much else. It's too easy to change an app now. Like Collison, you can just ask ChatGPT to do what you want and make you an app. Apple currently makes it a bit annoying, but believe me it's miles easier than before.

Once you start changing an app, it's hard to stop. You can see how it could be better so easily, and suddenly it is better. Even stuff that uses proprietary stuff often has an API, and if you trust the AI enough it can handle it for you.

Because this will never get less easy, apps will lose their identity and merge together. If you can change an app to do whatever you want, all the apps end up with the same purpose: do what I want. Show what I want. Most people around the world don't even have a laptop. 96% of people use smartphones. All they care is that the computer does what they want, and AI finally provides that.

Programming was hard enough before AI that we couldn't do it all that fast. So giving people what they want was just too hard, and too late.

Right now, the closest thing to a superapp is a browser like Safari or Chrome, and it's much more locked down and limited than a lot of apps. But ChatGPT's Intelligent UI and ChatGPT/Claude plugins are already going in this direction. And they works great, let me tell you. I use a whiteboard in them all the time and it took literally like 3 minutes and one ask to get it. There's no barrier.

The AI companies are going for it, but even without them, it's on the favored path. They've started with the search bar, then chat, then the terminal, and now documents and slides with their versions of Google Docs. Each generation of the chat and coding window gets closer. Right now incorporating the browser is the most awkward, but I don't expect it to last, especially when you can code whole browsers up so fast now. Just today, the Ladybird browser hit alpha and OpenAI released Shurido, a Linux runtime. Ladybird vibeported their codebase to Rust and had some article I saw on Hacker News about it. So Servo's dream of a new browser (in Rust) has been realized, but in a way I doubt they saw coming.

I don't know for a fact but I'm sure Shurido is vibecoded.

You can have a fluid interface in an app that just changes, but the natural limit of this --which is not far at all-- is just having the app change itself on the fly. Is it one app or is it many? They've kind of merged into a continuum. We're used to discrete separate apps, and now they've all merged together, the superapp.

Will there be specialization and separate apps? Not for most people. Maybe for a small group (not that I expect such sentences to survive ASI undistorted).

But the vast majority of people, normal people, want their phone to "just work".

So there's a phase transition from discrete apps to a continuous superapp, flowing like water. An app that adapts to show whatever's needed at any moment. And once you have that, what's the point of a separate operating system? It's just the App. Your phone/computer/BCI are hooks into the App. I don't expect merging interfaces between different people to be a blocker.

Once you have this, the devices themselves become less important but purer. Even more utilitarian. Their hardware could matter, but for most it won't. Even now the new Macbooks exceed what most people use them for. Until the AI taps that, their spare compute will just lie fallow.

I'm curious what the numbers for indie app revenue are these days. I expect the biggest apps to make even more money than before, but for the rest to shrink hard. Why pay $15 for some niche thing when I could ask the AI? Apps outside the 99 cent or Disney+ range are often for specialists, and programmer-adjacent people. Normal people hate paying for software.

I wonder how the next level will look. There's still value to showing cool ideas, even if us humans won't provide any groundbreaking ones ourselves. One AI could take a good design from another, and so on. In a way, the app ecosystem becomes more like a literal ecosystem, with copying acting like asexual reproduction. Or maybe more like asexual reproduction is copying copying.

Mindreading is a natural and creepy next step for these apps. After all, they could figure out what you wanna see before you even know it. Now that's low latency.

Like a lot of things in the future, I expect it to be awesome and kinda terrifying. I'm thinking of a rollercoaster drop right now, which is how I usually feel with AI. I know what's coming but I like the falling feeling because it's so fast and a helluva rush.

Whether or not AI kills us, I expect this to happen. More concretely, most --more than 50 percent-- of user app time is spent on a superapp. If we end up in the Matrix, that's the ultimate superapp of all.
