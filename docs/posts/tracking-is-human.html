tracking is human

25 Nov, 2026

last updated 2 days ago

i wrote about quantified self in a previous post during this writing month, and whilst i mostly stand by what i said, i do think i was a little harsh on something that has been a stalwart friend to me during hard (and good!) times.

i have (not enough!) pictures of lists i made, and (spotty!) .TXT file versions of journals i wrote in 2012 and thereafter, expounding my plans and hopes and dreams and dating troubles of the time, and though i cringe at them often they are also a gift!

i'm so glad i kept a little record, i definitely don't have enough pictures to sate my curiosity of any year of my life. i know from reading other blogs there may not be an "enough" that would realistically exist, but still, "barely any" to "none" is the sum total of photos i have from many periods of my life pre-Facebook, depressingly.

it seems deeply and unendingly human to track and capture and document our days. we keep the time, keeping our clocks and computers aligned. some of the up to half of us who bleed monthly track menstrual cycles, with paper or app or moon cycle if we're particularly regular or witchy! humanity has been tracking the moon and stars to work out where the hell we are for a very long time, and to work out what the hell the sky is doing for less time but with similar vigor! anniversaries! year starts/ends! birthdays!

the science in the carrier bag theory of fiction, a short essay by ursula le guin, is maybe a little dubious science wise, but i love the idea that humans use containers, and that is what makes us, us. i think there are so many things that could claim that spot; language or language facilitated communication (in relation to AI in linked youtube video) is a big one. i know other animals communicate, other animals use containers, and other animals can count (surely the precursor to tracking?) but i don't think any of this has to be uniquely human to be deeply human.

i do think there's an (also deeply human) tendency to say that one can only improve things that are trackable, or to assume that things that aren't quantifiable in any easy way don't "count" or matter. i think that's what i was reacting against, as i love a qualitative measure so much. there's nothing wrong with data driven decision making used wisely i guess, it just often feels like trying to squeeze my soft and squishy human body into a rigid and uncomfortable mechanical trap.

anyway. with this more expansive definition of tracking in place, i would love to know, is there anything you don't track you would like to? is there anything you track or commemorate or regularly you think is unusual? let me know!
