A Bold Claim

The restaurant business is a tough business to be in, but there are better restaurants than others.

In any case, I am not going to name the place, but there’s a specific place in New York that’s really terrible.

I’ve been honing/AI’ing around the question of “what is my taste?” and feeding models my starred locations, my hot and cold takes, etc. I kept talking about this specific joint as “this is the examplar bad place!”

I also fetched data, from the Google Mapses and elsewhere. I had data.

And I think that I found a (new-ish to me, but probably very old trick) pattern in review inflation. I have no inside knowledge of how any restaurant is run. Here’s what I think happens at places like this:

  1. A place is terrible.
  2. The terrible place starts getting negative reviews for how annoying it is. Note here that it’s usually interesting to see that bad places won’t spend too much controlling bad reviews on every site - and so bad reviews might accumulate on Yelp, but solicited-good-reviews will appear on Google Maps — yes, that GMaps vs Yelp delta is already a signal, maybe. But there’s a funnier one…
  3. To compensate for the bad reviews, the manager(s) tell staff: you gotta ask people to leave 5 star reviews!
  4. Either because the management doesn’t trust the staff, or because the staff gets incentivized in some way (or negatively incentivized, I picture something like “you’ll get fired if you don’t get 10 reviews posted every day”), management says: every time you ask people to post reviews, they gotta name your name
  5. Staff panicks (or makes more money, although I doubt it…) and pressures/cajoles people to leave 5 star reviews naming their names (i.e. the servers’ names)
  6. Greg looks at the data, points AI at it, and finds out that…

The horrible place’s most recent ~100 reviews (as of November 2026) name a server’s name 84% of the time and 95% of those were 5 stars!

Hahahahahahahahahahahha.

Brother! Those are not reviews, those are bird droppings served as Tiramisu!

Hahahhahahaha

I’m still not telling you the place’s name, but my God.
