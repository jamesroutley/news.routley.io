Some unorthodox flexitarian tips that I cannot recommend to anyone

27 Nov, 2026

If you worry about animal suffering, you know that factory-farmed meat and animal products are truly horrific. The simple, intuitive response is to become vegan to reduce your personal contributions to the cosmic horror. Unfortunately, that sounds hard, and you need something sustainable. Maybe it's even bad for your health, and you need to eat some meat, sometimes.

If you're playing that game, now the question becomes how you eat meat while still doing your part to reduce animal suffering. While there are some modest frameworks out there like Ameliatarianism or boring-but-arguably-highly-effective ideas like donating to animal welfare charities, there are a lot of unpopular sources of alpha that can take you further.

Without further ado, here are some:

Eat under-valued and under-desired meats

Society has specific tastes which create disproportionate demand for specific animal parts, while other parts of the same animal go to waste or find other uses. At the margin, this probably kills additional animals - if the only part of the chicken that people wanted to eat was the wing, we'd need to factory farm a lot more chickens! Efficient markets partially correct for this through price signals, but many succulent cuts are still left on the table.

You can fight back by specifically seeking out locally yucked animal parts for your protein sources. This could be organ meat, exceptionally tough cuts, or perhaps very fatty cuts. It really depends on the trends in your locale, so let low prices guide you. If you eat meat specifically because it's challenging to get enough iron or B12 otherwise, beef liver may be a genuinely optimal food.

Cat and dog food represent a conveniently homogenized form of this, which contain a lot of meat from industrial waste streams which are considered below human grade, but are still apparently perfectly edible and nutritious. As in Ameliatarianism, prefer beef over poultry or seafood when eating pet food.

Become a human garbage disposal

There's a lot of edible meat out there that is not even on the market. If you would be so courageous as to eat it when others won't, you could have your animal protein guilt-free!

No, I'm not talking about roadkill or maritime cannibalism, which are far too irregular to be relied upon. I'm talking about normal leftovers that are destined for the bin. Dumpster diving is rather pedestrian but remains under-leveraged.

There are also social settings that reliably produce vast amounts of edible waste. Restaurants throw out half-finished plates every day. When dining in a big group, never order meat for yourself, and always eagerly raise your hand when it comes time to pack away the leftovers, since someone is probably going to refuse to take theirs.

Any sort of catered event or party will also generate a lot of waste, since it involves preparing a huge amount of food without asking the devouring counterparts what they actually want to eat. One time I was at the office and I walked past a cocktail hour that had steamer trays full of meat skewers, sliders and sausages, that were mostly untouched because everyone at the cocktail hour was also invited to a dinner immediately after. It wasn't much, but I did my part.

A key part of this strategy is having adequate capacity to store and preserve any finds. Consider getting a chest freezer and carrying around a stack of empty tupperware to enable full exploitation.

Lose weight to reduce your protein requirements

There is a daily metabolic cost to being alive, and the more you do, weigh and eat, the more protein is required! There is a simple lesson here: reduce your food intake, reduce your physical activity, and try to lose lean mass. This will be challenging - consider GLP-1s or similar peptides to at least to get through the adjustment period - but at the end of it, you will require significantly less protein for homeostasis, up to 0.8 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight lost, especially if you are needlessly athletic.

One potential downside is that caloric restriction is thought to bring improvements to health and longevity, meaning that you may end up consuming more animals over the course of your extended lifespan! More research is needed to quantify the tradeoffs.

Threaten yourself financially

Common utilitarian wisdom implies that all else being equal, being vegan or vegetarian with a few cheat days is better than being fully omnivorous. However, it comes with terrible cognitive dissonance: you continue to crave and enjoy eating meat, but you know that you shouldn't. This makes it only quasi-stable as a strategy, and may not be sustainable over a lifetime.

However, you can use goal tracking and accountability tools such as Beeminder to set yourself a specific budget for carnivorous meals. When the chart looks green, you eat meat. When it looks yellow or red, you don't. The punishment for non-compliance is an exponentially scaling fine, so you can focus on your fully aligned motivations of maintaining financial well-being, and only contemplate animal suffering to the extent you can mentally tolerate.
