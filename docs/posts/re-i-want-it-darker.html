re: i want it darker

21 Nov, 2026

last updated 3 days, 19 hours ago

brennan wrote a personal essay today that really struck me, even if i don't agree with a lot of it! i thought i'd write a reply, so forgive me me being (even) more rambly than usual.

i want it darker and i want it brighter and i want it better and i want it more beautiful. let me explain.

darker?

i wouldn't go as far as to say my eyes are fully photophobic but given mental health stuff that is sensitive to light to the point i'm contraindicated from SAD lamps and daylight alarms it's fair to say i'm a little opposed to the brightness of summer. people have laughed at me having my laptop screen a few brightness clicks above "off", or lingering in my bedroom with the curtains closed during "glorious" sunshine.

brighter?

i get the urge to want to see everything burn; not that brennan is saying that (or even secretly feels it lol), but i see it a lot in the posts and feelings of other people, especially in the anti-AI space.

i get the urge to want to see oligarchs suffer and empires crumble. i understand why there's a longing for the apocalypse. i feel it on some levels, save for the literal and practical. every apocalypse fantasy is steeped in imaginary blood. for some reason even knowing that our current world was grown to its current unevenly lush abundance through the spilling of countless pints of blood itself i have little appetite for yet another rending.

the fire (of anger?) isn't the only light though. it would be nice to not blot out the stars with satellites any more than we already have, to be able to cast light on the hype and the real advancements we have made with technology, how much this will be able to change us. to actually be able to see everything clearly, in the clear light of day.

better?

i'm not much of a prepper. i think if something happens that means i have need of extensive electricity backups and food supply sources and perhaps even agriculture and engineering supplies i've stockpiled, i am fine with being a casualty of the transition. i think that 3 to 7 days of independent survival, as various governments have recommended as per the article brennan shared, feels ludicrous to me. i'm shocked how many people have such an attachment to life that they're cool to go along with it. not saying that this is healthy, honestly, but its my truth lol.

i get what some friends say, that a burst pipe here or there or a dark sky event could mean help would be slow to arrive, and three days of water or whatever would be a convenience. "normal" life could still resume if i could last the days until then? i think maybe i would be more happy if this was "community preparedness", if my street or area could band together and store water and other stuff as a group so it doesn't fall to individuals having the individual fortitude/private madness (depending on who you ask) to stock water purification methods or whatever.

beautiful?

i'm fine with being shaped by my tools, be they spectacles or taps or smartphones. we can't go back and unshape ourselves, maybe only forward into this dark/bright/beautiful/ugly and new world.

i don't think/feel there's virtue in suffering, just as there is no virtue in feeling good. we all suffer and we all die, and the majority of us have good moments and feelings somewhere in there. surrender to suffering i can get behind; it is going to happen anyway. i shall link to the tumblr post that is my last word on suffering.

this too shall pass was my mantra through the hard years of madness and in my case university. everything passes — save for the heat death of the universe maybe, but it's far away, and until then — and everything dies. including the bad and painful times. including our technologies and beautiful monuments. including our loves one and hated ones and all those we'll never know.
