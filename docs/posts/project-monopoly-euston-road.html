MONOPOLY PROPERTY NUMBER 4: EUSTON ROAD

(Details of books and maps at the end of the post)

Technically speaking, the order of the light blue properties if following a map east to west should run like this: The Angel Islington, Pentonville Road, Euston Road. King’s Cross Station sits where Pentonville and Euston Roads meet.

I am following the order of the streets as laid down on the Monopoly board so will continue with Euston Road. After exploring the area around Angel tube station, I took a bus south from Upper Street, turning right along Pentonville Road and then going further on to Euston Road. I will write about this in a future post for anyone who wishes to cover the three light blue Monopoly properties on the same day.

 

 

HOW TO GET HERE

For transport options, Citymapper is invaluable as a tool to get from A to B. Several tube stations serve the Euston Road: Great Portland Street, Warren Street, Euston Square and King’s Cross St Pancras. There are several bus routes that go along the road as well.

Driving along Euston Road has its own hazards, as it is on the edge of the London Congestion Charge zone. Whilst drivers are not charged for travelling on the road, if they inadvertantly turn south and breach the zone during its long hours of operation a heavy charge of £18 ensues.

 

 

ASPECTS OF MY VISIT

As the Euston Road is a major road (part of the London Inner Ring Road) it is not a road to wander along for shopping or dining. However there are several grand buildings along this road worth looking out for including King’s Cross train station, St Pancras train station, St Pancras London Hotel, the British Library, Friends House (national HQ of the Quakers), the former Elizabeth Garrett Anderson Hospital, the Royal College of General Practitioners and the Wellcome Collection.

The Wellcome Collection

This was my first visit to the Wellcome Collection and I thoroughly recommend it. My favourite item in the permanent display is an anatomical model of a woman whose organs light up when you press a panel of buttons. It never ceases to amaze me how many body parts need to fit onto the cavity of our (bodily) trunk.
