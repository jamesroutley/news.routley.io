The Sicilian Citrus Family Tree

The Citrus Kingdom · Epilogue

Every citrus fruit in Sicily descends from four wild fruits of Asia. Fruit sketches hand-drawn by the author.

By Erik Gauger · November 9, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

This chart is the epilogue to The Citrus Kingdom, an eight-part series on how citrus, carried west from Asia, shaped Sicily’s politics, culture and cuisine for a thousand years. This is how its fruits are related.

Roman numerals mark each fruit’s chapter in The Citrus Kingdom. Tap a fruit to read it. A dotted line is a sport: one branch of one tree that mutated, the way the late mandarin of Ciaculli began. The bottom three rows are not new crosses either: they are selections and mutations of the sweet orange and the lemon. The full hand-drawn Citrus of Sicily plate is below.

  1. Citron, pomelo and papeda are wild ancestors, all from Asia. Part I, Palermo · Part V, Vendicari
  2. Sour orange: pomelo × mandarin, an ancient cross made in Asia. Part II, Cefalù
  3. Interdonato: a lemon–citron hybrid, bred in Sicily. Part III, Lipari
  4. Sweet orange: pomelo × mandarin, crossed back to mandarin, in Asia. Tarocco, Moro and Sanguinello, the three blood oranges of Etna, are red mutations of it; Tarocco and Moro arose from Sanguinello in Syracuse province, in eastern Sicily. Part IV, Taormina
  5. Verdello: a Femminello lemon forced into a summer crop. Part VI, Ortigia
  6. Femminello: Sicily’s main lemon. Monachello and Lunario are its neighbors; Limone dell’Etna is Femminello and Monachello grown on the volcano. Part VII, Modica
  7. Tardivo di Ciaculli: the late mandarin, a sport of an Avana mandarin tree in Ciaculli, near Palermo, in the 1940s, ripening from January into March. Part VIII, Conca d’Oro
  8. Mandarino: the Mediterranean mandarin, called Avana in Sicily, grown in Italy since the early 1800s. Part VIII, Conca d’Oro Clementine: mandarin × sweet orange, from Algeria, around 1900.
  9. Lemon: sour orange × citron, a hybrid of a hybrid. Where it first arose is uncertain.
  10. Biondo Comune, Ovale, Valencia, Navelina and Ribera: the blond sweet oranges grown in Sicily. Part IV, Taormina
  11. Bergamot: sour orange × lemon, grown in Calabria; its origin is disputed. Chinotto: a mutation of the sour orange.
  12. Cedrino: a smaller, historically recognized form of the cedro, the citron.
  13. Pompia: sour orange × citron, from Sardinia. Lumia: an old citron hybrid, most often citron × pomelo.
  14. Key lime: papeda × citron, from Southeast Asia. Persian lime: Key lime × lemon; its place of origin is unknown. Grapefruit: sweet orange × pomelo, from Barbados in the 18th century.

Sources: Wu et al., Nature (2018); Curk et al., Annals of Botany (2016); Luro et al., Phytochemistry (2019).
