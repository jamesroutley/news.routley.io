on being mid

23 Nov, 2026

last updated 4 days ago

"i have found that battling despair does not mean closing my eyes to the enormity of the tasks of effecting change, nor ignoring the strength and the barbarity of the forces aligned against us. it means teaching, surviving and fighting with the most important resource i have, myself, and taking joy in that battle. it means, for me, recognizing the enemy outside and the enemy within, and knowing that my work is part of a continuum of women’s work, of reclaiming this earth and our power, and knowing that this work did not begin with my birth nor will it end with my death. and it means knowing that within this continuum, my life and my love and my work has particular power and meaning relative to others." - audre lorde, the cancer journals

i just watched a youtube video: it's okay to be mid and chopped and broke, tw: weight loss chat. i don't particularly care about the "chopped" part of this, but i find the idea interesting.

the saying, though it makes little astronomical sense (to be all neil degrasse tyson for a second) is "aim for the moon, and you'll land amongst the stars". i think often it should be more like “aim for the stars, but in all likelihood you are not gonna end up more than a couple hours from here, and you know what? that's good, AND okay! we need people here too, and even if we didn't, you matter, your happiness matters, and you can make where you land a home".

i've mentioned in some of my weeknotes struggling with the tension between wanting to do exceptional things, accepting that most people can't or won't be exceptional, my strong though nascent belief in the sacredness of the everyday, and also my ailing and slowly dying belief that if i made a metric TON of money i could do a lot of good. here, a ton of money is at least a billion and a ton of money is at least more than any one person should reasonably be able to do.

i think part of accepting my relative smallness is accepting our collective smallness in the face of mortality and the universe and time, but also our towering collective stature in the face of injustice (...if we could all just agree what injustice is and how to fight it, and recognise said power, and and and).

it's not just okay to be mid, it's actively a good thing. i want the median, middle, average, normal person to have a good life, and not good like exceptionally, good in an every life way, and they shouldn't have to do anything out of pocket to earn that.

how do you feel about being average? does it immediately put your back up (guilty still over here lol)? let me know!
