trying really hard

archive about contact guestbook projects now ai notes tip me more let there be light (light mode)

26 Nov, 2026

  • nolan's great post, legibility of effort, and many other posts and conversations about AI making certain kinds of work easier has really been on my mind recently. making effort legible is an interesting way to frame the problem; i dunno if things like devlogs, cool sites like inprogress.works and the rise and rise of art/coding/writing/etc "process" content (even before LLMs were on the scene) speaks to this thirst in some people.
    • i watched a great youtube video, i don't care if AI steals my game on how, sure, making a devlog might mean that the idea and "core" (huge scare quotes) idea for a game might be stolen, but that the human element, and the natural differences in interpretation of what a mechanic means, lead to wildly disparate end products. aka, you are what people want when you make a game, not just the game in itself!
  • another post i read, how to try hard by mason currey (please excuse the substack!), really inspired me to think about how hard i try in general, i think i lack some vital thing that makes it easy to try as hard as i can on things i care about, maybe called ADHD but you know!!!! gotta give ourselves grace. i think knowing that i tried as hard as i could, albeit given a huge list of variables and internal dispositions does help with said grace.
  • trying hard is unfashionable and "tryhard" is something you call someone to try and make them feel smaller. effort is only valued if it's seen as the only way to do something often? effort for its own sake is often the domain of hobbyists and other fun kooks.
  • a common anti-LLM refrain online at the moment is "i can't be bothered to read it if you couldn't be bothered to write it".
    • i agree with this. though "you didn't try very hard to write this, so i am not gonna try very hard to read it" doesn't hold the same persuasive power, sometimes slapdash or off the cuff writing/creations often have other qualities that make them engaging, or, horror of horrors, maybe the audience is unable to tell something didn't take very long! maybe the creator has the skill that their twenty minute doodle is better than something a beginner to the craft spent a whole week on!

i was going to ask a question here like "do you prefer when people work hard on the art/sites/writing/etc you engage with?" but that feels like a leading question, so instead i'll ask: how do you make your effort legible to yourself and others? does it matter to you at all? let me know!

this is my twenty sixth post in my writing month challenge, which i decided is "one post for each day of november".
