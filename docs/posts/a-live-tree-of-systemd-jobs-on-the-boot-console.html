A live tree of systemd jobs on the boot console

A live tree of systemd jobs on the boot console, in the spirit of nix-output-monitor. It replaces the scrolling kernel and systemd lines with the units still pending and, in the room the tree leaves, a timeline of the slowest ones that ran.

I started bootvine out of frustration with the boot screen of most Linux distributions: a wall of log lines scrolling past too fast to read, which never gave me any idea of what was going on.

It also follows shutdowns, answers passphrase prompts such as a LUKS unlock in place, and shows why a unit failed with its last journal lines. The guide walks through each part of the screen.

Requirements

  • systemd ≥ 246 as PID 1
  • libdbus and libsystemd
  • a virtual terminal
  • a systemd initrd, to show the boot from the initrd as well as stage 2

See Install for each distribution.

Try it without installing

With Nix, boot a sample NixOS machine with slow and failing units in a QEMU window, without touching your system's boot:

nix run git+https://codeberg.org/wvhulle/bootvine#sample-vm-slow-services

It logs in as root; poweroff shows the shutdown. The guide lists the other sample machines and the scripted demos that play in a terminal.
