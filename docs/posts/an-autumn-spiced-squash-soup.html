An Autumn Spiced Squash Soup

 

 

This week I had a vegetable box delivery with some squash included: Queen Squash, Kabocha Squash and Acorn Squash. Being short of time I had not thought of anything beyond cutting and roasting the squash. I started with the Queen Squash (2.5 kilos) then found that it was too much squash for us to tackle, even with the help of Junior 2.

I had in the fridge some leftover coconut milk and a bottle of the most intensely and insanely salty Thai tom yum paste. I could not eat the soup I made with it. When I read the small print, there was 16 g of salt per 100 g of paste. There should be a health warning on the label. I was about to throw this away but then thought to use it as a seasoning.

So I made a squash soup. It was delicious and I made it twice, the second time with the Acorn Squash. It is really delicious and you should try it too.

 

Squash in season (top) and an Acorn Squash (below)

 

In order to make the soup it is necessary to roast the squash first. Simply cut the squash into wedges, brush the exposed flesh with some olive oil, put the tray in the oven on a medium-high heat and roast until soft. The instructions are purposefully vague, as all squash have different density of flesh. I think it is best to cook something else in the oven at the same time e.g. a cake, a tray of choc chip cookies, some foil-wrapped beetroot/ sweet potato or a whole chicken.

 

 

This would be a good recipe to make with leftover roasted root vegetables from a roast lunch. With varying degrees of sweetness and starchiness, you could also substitute sweet potato, parsnip, cauliflower or carrots for the squash.

 

For the Soup:

(All vegetables are peeled weight)

100 g white onion, leek (white part) or fennel, sliced thickly

20 g fresh root ginger, sliced

15 g garlic cloves

20 g sunflower or olive oil

2 teaspoons hot curry powder

3 kaffir lime leaves, centre stem removed and sliced

500 g cooked squash (see note above)

430 g unsalted chicken or vegetable stock

200 g coconut milk (I use Aroy D brand)

1 tablespoon tom yum paste or any other East Asian spice paste

 

I used my Thermomix but it would be equally easy to simmer the soup in a pot then blend it with a hand-help electric blender.

 

How to Make:

Place the onion (or leek/ fennel), garlic and ginger in the TM mixing bowl. Chop 6 seconds/ speed 5.

Scrape down the sides of the TM bowl with a silicon spatula (I never use the TM spatula).

Add the oil and curry powder. Sauté 5 minutes/ 100°C/ speed 1.

Add the cooked squash and vegetable or chicken stock (which has to be unsalted). Simmer 15 minutes/ 100°C/ speed 1.

Add the coconut milk and curry paste. Blend 45 seconds/ speed 10 (start with speed 4 then dial up the speed to 10 gradually).

Taste for seasoning, pour into some bowls and serve with any seasonal topping available: chopped herbs, herb oil, toasted seeds and nuts. Toasted sourdough may be all you need on the side.
