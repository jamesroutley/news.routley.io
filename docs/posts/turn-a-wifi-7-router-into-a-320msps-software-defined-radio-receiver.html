Turn a WiFi 7 Router into a 320Msps Software Defined Radio Receiver

Turns a Gemtek W1700K Wi-Fi 7 router into a wideband software-defined receiver. The router's MediaTek MT7996 radio captures raw 14-bit IQ at up to 320 Msps, its Airoha AN7581 SoC streams it over 10 GbE, and a Linux host shows it as a live spectrum and waterfall in the browser, with the receiver's controls next to it.

The whole 2.4 GHz band at 320 Msps, live, with the decimation's roll-off corrected in the stream ("flatten"): a 262,144-point FFT of every sample, 100 spectra a second (this recording runs at 50 frames a second).

The MT7996 runs only our own firmware: a ROM patch, WM and WA built from this repository, with no MediaTek code. It brings the radio up from a cold power-on by itself, from MediaTek's initialisation and calibration decoded register by register against a trace of MediaTek's own firmware, and takes its commands (LO, sample rate, capture, stream, gain, filter) from the host. See docs/architecture.md and docs/roadmap.md.
Sample rates 40, 80, 160 or 320 Msps of 14-bit complex IQ on every band, independent of the LO and the filter; on band 2 also 640/2 (every other sample of 640 Msps: flat to ±155 MHz, but signals 160–320 MHz away fold in)
Bands 2.4 GHz (MT7976), 5 GHz and 6 GHz (two MT7977s); switching bands is a register write inside a running stream
Tuning No channels: the LO is any Hz a band's synthesizer locks at (unit A: 2.081–2.748, 4.647–7.509 and 4.707–7.576 GHz), on its grid of 2.98 Hz (2.4 GHz) or 11.9–14.3 Hz steps; retuned mid-stream (about 1 ms)
Front end AGC or manual LNA/baseband gain; analog filter 20–320 MHz
Transport the radio DMAs into a 1 GiB ring in SoC DRAM; the kernel sends every 8 KiB slice as one UDP datagram, zero-copy, on the 10 GbE port; at 320 Msps half the slices are packed to 28 bits per sample pair on the radio's own cores to fit the link (lossless, automatic)
Host w1700k-stream receives the export and serves the IQ stream to every consumer on the host (shared memory, complex int16, gap-tagged), with an optional correction ("flatten") of the decimation's roll-off applied to the samples themselves; w1700k-spectrum: 262,144-point FFT at 100 frames/s over every sample, browser UI, live controls; sdr-recv records the stream

2.4 GHz at 320 Msps, flattened: Wi-Fi frames and shorter hopping bursts on a floor that stays level to the band's edges. The Stream panel shows the link carrying 9.75 Gbit/s, half of the slices packed, and every sample transformed.

Two busy 80 MHz Wi-Fi channels at 5170–5330 MHz in a flattened 320 MHz view.

Zoomed to 24 MHz around 5250 MHz: the two channels' edges and the guard gap between them, in 1.22 kHz bins.

You need a W1700K, a 3.3 V USB serial adapter on its console header, an Ethernet cable to its lan3 port for installation, and a 10 GbE NIC on the host cabled to its wan port (10GBASE-T; an X710 with a copper SFP+ module works) for the IQ stream.

make                                   # everything, from public sources (~10 min plus downloads)
make check                             # the test suite

sudo init/host/link up IFACE           # 192.168.1.10/24 on the NIC cabled to lan3
sudo init/host/tftp-forward enable IFACE
init/install                           # back up the NAND, install, save the radio calibration

sudo init/host/link up IFACE10G 10.77.0.10/24 9216    # the 10 GbE stream link
host/stream/build/w1700k-stream &                      # the stream, for every consumer on this host
host/spectrum/build/w1700k-spectrum --controller "host/bin/w1700k serve" --listen 0.0.0.0:8073

Then open http://HOST:8073/ and press ▶. The first start loads the radio firmware and brings the radio up (about 2 s). Without the browser, the same operations are host/bin/w1700k load|start|band|tune|gain|filter|stop|status.

Build requirements: a C/C++ compiler, make, git, Python 3.10+, the usual Buildroot prerequisites, clang, ld.lld, llvm-objcopy and llvm-readobj (RISC-V firmware), openssl (firmware container encryption), and libfftw3-dev and zlib for the stream service and the spectrum server. No vendor firmware is downloaded or needed.

  • Our firmware is receive-only. It runs none of MediaTek's transmit calibrations and keeps the MAC's transmit path disabled; a gapless watch at 5250 MHz saw nothing during a load, where MediaTek's firmware transmits a full-power burst. Still, do not cable an antenna port straight into a sensitive instrument without an attenuator.
  • Our ROM patch writes no eFuse. MediaTek's ROM patches since 2025 permanently program one eFuse gate bit on their first run (also with MediaTek's own driver); a unit that ever ran one has that bit set.
  • Installation replaces the stock operating system. init/install first copies the whole NAND to the host; keep that copy. Restoring stock needs a raw NAND writer, which this repository does not provide.
  • Accessing undocumented MT7996 or AN7581 addresses can hang the SoC or wedge the radio until a power cycle. include/hw/ marks every known hazard.
