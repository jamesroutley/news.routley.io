11 UI improvements in otel-desktop-viewer

The hyperfocus gods have been generous to me lately, but switching activities is hard, so most of that energy has gone directly into otel-desktop-viewer, and very little none has gone into writing about it. I’m working on longer posts about agent support (that I shipped without telling anyone, sorry 🇨🇦) and upgrading to DuckDB 2 (a native VARIANT type y’all).

In the meantime, here’s a tour of UI improvements!

1. Look! A new theme!

I loved the previous colour scheme (Rosé Pine), but accessibility is one of my priorities for otel-desktop-viewer, and meeting WCAG’s text contrast requirements was a struggle.

The replacement, Madoromi, gets its colours from scans of Pilot Iroshizuku fountain pen inks. It’s gorgeous, and its consistent colour roles, palette generation and contrast checks make it easier to build the same UI for light and dark mode.

You can switch between four seasons, each with Day and Night variants, without changing your service or series colours.

2. Read structured attributes

I started coming across span attributes as JSON strings (looking at you gen-ai semantic conventions) and reading them as raw strings felt gross. The details pane now shows their structure with indentation, syntax highlighting and folding. Below is the same JSON string in v0.5.0 (left) and v0.7.0 (right), with the retries array folded.

The same view works for native OTLP arrays and maps, represented in OTLP JSON by arrayValue and kvlistValue, respectively. Below, agent.tool_calls on the left is an array of maps, while agent.context on the right is a map containing nested maps and an array.

2′ Search within maps and arrays

Okay so this isn’t entirely a UI feature, but I’m sneaking it in anyway because it’s neat. Let’s call it 2′.

You can now query individual values inside maps and arrays. Let’s say you wanted to find agent runs where the second tool call reported at least three retries:

span.attributes["agent.context"]["tool_calls"]
  [1]["result"]["retries"]::BIGINT >= 3

3. Copy and download OTLP JSON

Ever wish you could see the raw OTLP payload behind a value? Now you can! The JSON tab shows the selected record as OTLP JSON, with its resource and scope context. You can fold the JSON, copy it or download it as a file.

You can now bring those OTLP files back into the viewer: drop JSON or JSONL files anywhere in the window. Send your colleague a weird trace and they can drag it right in to see what you see.

5. Compare metric series

The new series table lets you sort and compare series by their attributes, such as CPU core. Hover over a row to highlight its line on the chart, or click it to inspect the datapoints. You can select individual datapoints from either the chart or the table.

6. Explore heatmaps and histograms

You can highlight p50, p95 and p99 on the heatmaps while keeping the full distribution visible. This helps you see whether requests are getting slower across the board or just in the tail.

You can also hide the overlays to inspect the distribution on its own.

The histogram matches the heatmap’s colours for bucket counts. Selecting a datapoint shows how much it contributes to each bucket in the combined distribution. Texture marks the selected datapoint’s contribution.

7. See reporting gaps

Gauge charts now leave gaps when samples stop arriving, while keeping real zero readings on the chart.

8. Find logs and events in the trace timeline

Span events and correlated logs now appear together in the waterfall (both are invited to the Span Event API deprecation party). Hover over the circles to see span activity, with nearby markers sharing a popover. Choose an event to inspect its attributes, or follow a correlated log to its full record.

9. Add new columns to the trace waterfall view

Spans are full of good information! When you are looking at a trace you might want more info than just the span name and service. For example, add the agent’s phase and step number as columns to compare them across spans without opening each one.

10. Customize columns and inspect structured logs

Similar to the trace view, you can now also customize your columns in the log view! The table also has severity filters and error navigation. Select a log to inspect its body and expand nested data, such as the tool result and error details below. Use Collapse all and Expand all to fold or unfold the whole body at once.

11. Explore telemetry from the service map

If you’re running your own fleet of services during development, the service map on Home gives you a quick way to find the latest telemetry from a particular service. Click a service to browse the telemetry it emitted in the details pane. The results follow your current search and time range.

Other niceties

  • Navigation moved to the top bar, leaving more room for your data.
  • Scroll-to-top buttons in the logs table, log details and trace waterfall.
  • Copy buttons for individual fields and whole records.
  • Resizable panes and columns.
  • Arrow-key navigation between tabs in the details pane.
  • Jump between log errors, including across pages.
  • Both ends of long IDs stay visible in narrow waterfall columns.
  • Readable storage sizes, with exact byte counts on hover and Copy.
  • Choose your own colours for individual services and metric series. Change service colours directly from the map on Home.

Try it out

Install otel-desktop-viewer, point an OTLP exporter at it and your telemetry shows up in the browser. Everything shown here is in 0.7.0.
