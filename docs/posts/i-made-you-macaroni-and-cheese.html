I Made You Macaroni and Cheese

Hello everyone!

It’s Thanksgiving time, and if you’ve spent the last two years hoping to hear me announce that I’m bringing Home Movies back, this is about to be the worst day of your life because: I’m not. But! If you’ve been waiting two years to hear me yap (on camera) while I cook food (in a new, different, fun format), then this is about to be the best day of your life. Introducing…Having People Over. 

This new project—it’s a podcast! It’s a YouTube show!—is a continuation of the Nothing Fancy: Unfussy Food for Having People Over legacy. In that book, I wanted to reframe modern gatherings, to remind you that cooking for others doesn’t have to be stressful, elaborate or difficult. “It’s not entertaining…it’s having people over.” 

Which is exactly what I’m going to do. In each episode, I invite a friend into my kitchen, and while I cook them something wonderful we answer your questions about hosting, about life, about love, about chicken, probably. (I can’t believe how many questions people have about chicken.) Consider this a sort of cooking-forward VH1 Storytellers. It’s easy and casual, personal and stripped down—and also there’s an acoustic guitar.

Our first episode is all about Thanksgiving, and my guest is Bowen “Mary Todd Lincoln” Yang. Could you imagine anything better? I make him my favorite Thanksgiving things, and because I’m a person who believes in kindness during the holidays, I also make his favorite Thanksgiving thing: macaroni and cheese. 

You may or may not know or care that I don’t usually have macaroni and cheese on my Thanksgiving table. It’s nothing personal! While it is a top five favorite food of mine, come Thanksgiving, between the sour cream mashed potatoes and stuffing with celery and leeks, there just isn’t enough room (in my heart, mind or body). Every dish on the table has a role to play, and the starchy, creamy, crunchy-topped baked things quota has been filled. 

And yet! Here I am, publishing exactly one new recipe this Thanksgiving season and…it’s macaroni and cheese. I’m stubborn about my menu, but I’m also an overachieving People Pleaser, and if someone I love tells me their favorite part of Thanksgiving is macaroni and cheese…you know I’m making them the best macaroni and cheese they’ve ever had. 

So here she is. For Bowen. For everyone who has asked me for one since 2019. For you: a Holiday Macaroni and Cheese.

Rich and cozy while also perhaps “lighter than usual,” it’s perfectly salty and just cheesy enough. There’s buttermilk added to the bechamel for a lovely little tang, tons of black pepper to remind you of cacio e pepe and a whisper of nutmeg to remind you it’s The Holidays. There are four types of cheeses, all of which contribute something special and unique. (In the spirit of accessibility and flexibility, I give you a few chances to use only two or three, but four cheeses is sadly the correct number of cheeses here. It’s the holidays! Commit.) And, no, there aren’t any breadcrumbs, and this macaroni and cheese is better for it. It really is special, and I hope you agree. 

If you want to follow along, here’s everything I made for Bowen. It’s an assemblage of what I consider to be the most important things on the Thanksgiving table, a sort of “if you’re going to make anything, make these things” type of menu: 

  • Fennel Rubbed Turkey with Sheet Pan Gravy. My gold standard. 
  • Buttered Stuffing with Celery and Leeks. I can’t do better than this and I don’t want to. 
  • Sour Cream Mashed Potatoes. There are tons of chives and also lumps (on purpose). 
  • Cranberries and Honey. Two ingredients, perfect cranberry sauce. Add orange if you must. 
  • Holiday Macaroni and Cheese (recipe below!). There’s lots of black pepper, a whisper of nutmeg, four types of cheese and the secret addition of buttermilk. It’s very special to me.

My guest is someone who will make you want to put on a little fuzzy sweater, sip a warm autumnal beverage and curl up next to your favorite new podcast. After that, you can expect two more holiday episodes in December, and then weekly episodes beginning in the new year.

Holiday Macaroni and Cheese

Holiday Macaroni and Cheese
Holiday Macaroni and Cheese.pdf
682 KB
Serves 4 as a main, 6 as a side

This macaroni and cheese is less “weeknight” and more “holiday,” meant to be served at a time when it seems not only reasonable but perhaps desirable to eat a dish full of pasta and dairy alongside a large-format meat and tons of wine. Not even because it’s especially labor intensive, more that it feels, well…special. It has a lot going for it: four unique and fabulous cheeses (you can skip one and go all-in on the cheddar or Gruyère, but the texture and flavor will be rewarded if you use both), a generous amount of black pepper, a whisper of nutmeg and a bit of thyme sprinkled over to finish. (I know you’ll be tempted, but don’t skip the nutmeg. Just commit right now to buying the whole jar knowing you’ll never use it all, but that it’ll be worth it for this dish alone.) Plus, there’s the unmistakable tang—a small reprieve from all that richness—from buttermilk, my favorite not-so-secret ingredient, which gets added to the bechamel at the last minute.

To achieve the proper finished texture for both the cheese sauce and the pasta, you might notice it feels especially saucy going into the oven—this is by design. After it bakes, you’ll be rewarded with properly cooked noodles, sauce thickened just so and an impossibly gorgeous brown crust, nary a breadcrumb in sight. This makes plenty for a smaller gathering, but can of course be doubled quite easily for larger parties or leftovers insurance. (Eating cold, leftover macaroni and cheese, all tight and congealed, is a secret pleasure of mine).

This is decadence, this is joy, this is a celebration. This is…your holiday macaroni and cheese. 

Ingredients

  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour 
  • So much freshly ground black pepper
  • A whisper of finely grated nutmeg
  • 2 cups whole milk 
  • 4 ounces white cheddar, grated 
  • 4 ounces Gruyère, grated 
  • 6 ounces fresh mozzarella (from a ball), grated, divided
  • 1 large garlic clove, finely grated  
  • ½ cup buttermilk (or more milk)
  • Kosher salt
  • 8 ounces cavatappi or elbow macaroni
  • Parmesan for grating on top, optional
  • A few sprigs of thyme, optional

Preparation

  1. Preheat oven to 425°F. (If convection is an option, use it.)
  2. Melt butter in a medium pot over medium heat until starting to foam. Whisk in flour and cook, whisking constantly, until the roux is a pale shade of brown, 2–3 minutes. Add black pepper and nutmeg to toast the spices for 30 seconds or so.  
  3. Slowly whisk in milk until smooth and no lumps remain. Add cheddar, Gruyère, about ¾ mozzarella and garlic, whisking to melt and blend. 
  4. Add buttermilk (or more milk) and season well with salt and pepper. Remove from heat and set aside
  5. Cook pasta in a large pot of salted, boiling water until about halfway cooked (about half the time you usually boil your pasta—extremely al dente). Drain and shake off excess water as well as you can.
  6. Add pasta to the cheese mixture, stirring to coat very well, giving each noodle as much sauce exposure as it deserves. Transfer to a medium baking dish (an 8”x8” dish, 9” round cake pan or pie plate, an 8”–9” cast-iron pan, or anything approximately that size) and top with remaining mozzarella, Parmesan (if using), thyme (if using) and more black pepper (in that order). 
  7. Transfer to the oven and bake until pasta is bubbling at the edges and gorgeously browned on top, 30–35 minutes (closer to 20–25 minutes for convection). Remove from the oven and let sit at least 5 or so minutes before serving.
