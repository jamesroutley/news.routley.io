The Hallway Track No. 028: arXiv rate limit, AI paper mills, Google FluSight

arXiv's new submission cap and a post warning that the next generation of mathematics paper mills will produce correct proofs rather than fraudulent ones are responses to the same underlying change: AI makes it cheap to produce scientific outputs that are technically sound and voluminous at the same time. AI-Newton, a system that independently recovers Newton's second law and conservation of energy from raw experimental data with no physical knowledge supplied, is the clearest current illustration of what technically sound and automated looks like in practice. Rediscovering known results from data is a validation exercise, not a discovery, and the infrastructure for distinguishing the two has not changed.

Scholarly infrastructure

  • arXiv caps submissions at two per month in response to AI-driven volume

    arXiv Blog, October 1 2026

    arXiv now limits each submitter to two submissions per calendar month, citing AI-driven growth from roughly 9,900 submissions in September 2016 to roughly 40,400 in September 2026, with the AI category alone growing about sixfold over the past two years and the surge generating roughly 9,000 support tickets.

  • Introducing Routines: Research that keeps working

    Elicit, September 30 2026

    Elicit's Research Agent can now run literature-monitoring tasks on a daily or weekly schedule, returning updated summaries without requiring the researcher to re-run the same search manually.

Research automation

  • Google's science AI tops the CDC FluSight 2025-26 evaluation

    Google Research, September 30 2026

    Google's model ranked first among 39 eligible submissions in the CDC's 2025-26 FluSight evaluation for predicting flu hospital admissions on average weighted interval score across jurisdictions, a benchmark that has run since the 2013-14 influenza season.

  • AI-Newton: A Concept-Driven Physical Law Discovery System without Prior Physical Knowledge

    arXiv (You-Le Fang, Dong-Shan Jian, Xiang Li, Yan-Qing Ma), April 2025 (revised December 2025)

    A system that receives only raw experimental data and no physical knowledge independently recovers Newton's second law, conservation of energy, and universal gravitation from a large noisy mechanics dataset, validating the concept-driven approach in a test where all the correct answers were already known.

  • Claude for Science event at the Niels Bohr Institute

    Jacob Langvad Nilsson, LinkedIn, September 2026

    The University of Copenhagen's Niels Bohr Institute will host a November 12 event with four case studies of Danish researchers using Claude agents in active research workflows, including one group whose automated literature search identified the bacteriophage enzyme system; the post frames agent-based work as routine practice for which human validation at the end remains the scientist's responsibility.

  • Hackathon with autoresearch as a sponsored track

    Jonty Corrin, LinkedIn, September 28 2026

    A hackathon announced September 28 organizes autonomous research as one of four sponsored competitive tracks alongside drug design, protein design, and materials design, with over $100k in cash and credits on offer from sponsors including cthree.cloud, the AIDDA Institute, Serova, Polaron, Cognition, and Modal.

Authorship and publishing

  • A new type of paper mill

    Proofs and Prompts (Eric Dolores-Cuenca, Yonsei University), October 1 2026

    The threat Dolores-Cuenca describes is not fraudulent papers but correct ones: AI systems trained to identify important mathematical problems, solve them, and sell the outputs to researchers who submit with their names, a scenario that defeats AI-detection tools because the work is mathematically sound and can be rewritten before submission.

  • What about the preprints?

    Proofs and Prompts (Manuel Rissel, ShanghaiTech University), October 1 2026

    Rissel asks whether mathematicians want their preprints crawled by AI systems during the peer-review period, and proposes voluntary encryption as a mechanism for maintaining timestamped priority while restricting automated access to the body of the paper before an embargo expires.
