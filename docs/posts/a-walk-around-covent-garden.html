A Walk Around Covent Garden

 

 

As I walked towards Bow Street for my Project Monopoly, Mr Gochugaru had to remind me multiple times why we were here in the first place. Bow Street is in Covent Garden and with so many opportunities for eating and shopping I kept getting sidetracked.

We so enjoyed coming here when were students in the mid 1980s. Pat Chapman’s Curry Shop (giant samosas), Crank’s wholefood restaurant (cheese-topped baps filled with cress) and the Covent Garden General Store (cheap crockery and novelties) were our old haunts. I first shopped at David Mellor in their James Street store, and I own an array of their Leach pottery bowls which remind me of the bowls used in the Cranks restaurants.

This is a very popular tourist destination and here are a few places we visited over the weekend.

 

CAFES

There is no shortage of bakeries here for a small and big bites. Some restraint needs to be exercised. We had breakfast at Arôme Bakery, where there are constant queues. Birchwood at the Conduit has just opened. Fair Shot is a social enterprise cafe. We also bought a cardamom bun from Swedish bakery Fabrique to take home.

For other recommended coffee shops, European Coffee Trip is a useful guide. We are spoilt for choice.

“LOOK UP”

The Phoenix Garden is a community garden hidden from the hustle and bustle of the West End. We had a lovely chat with the events manager having met her previously. The grand-twins had their introduction-to-friends-and-family afternoon tea party here at the end of June. The weather was record-breaking hot and Old Chang Kee did us proud with their curry puffs.

At the time I could not take a photo of the “Look Up Bench” as there were many people about. I love Wham!, I love a good romance and so Last Christmas (the movie) is very much up my street. The bench features in the story.

This was a very invigorating morning as we walked around the streets of Covent Garden. This has given us encouragement to explore other parts of London over the summer. There is always a danger that you don’t visit your own city, and that would be a shame here in London.
