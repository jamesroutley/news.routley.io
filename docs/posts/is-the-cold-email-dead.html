Is the cold email dead?

When I just starting my career, I discovered a bunch of other engineers whose work inspired me. I saw their projects, read their blogs, and followed them on social media. The ones I connected with the most didn’t have the largest audience, but they cared about the same things that I did. They were my people.

I soon learned that I could just email them. If I did it right, they would reply, and just like that, I could connect to kindred spirits all over the world. It was an incredible feeling.

But it got better.

As I built my own things, shared my own thoughts, and put my own work out there… other kindred spirits started emailing me! I didn’t have a massive audience, but it didn’t matter. Receiving and replying to these personal emails was a rewarding experience, one described in detail by Henrik Karlsson in “A blog post is a very long and complex search query to find fascinating people and make them route interesting stuff to your inbox”.

Of course, I’ve received a lot of spam as well, but it was always easy to tell whether the email was a thoughtful message from a real human or a marketing campaign for some product or service.

I worry that those days may be over.

If you’re like me, you’ve recently started receiving tons of LLM-powered mass-customized emails that look a lot like the personal emails I used to love. Here are three recent examples—can you tell which are real and which are spam?

Hi Bryan,

Saw you launched Warby Parker’s AR virtual glasses try-on while at Nearform, which is a pretty fun piece of frontend work. Now that you’re at ClassDojo, I wanted to flag a role where that blend of polished customer-facing UX and serious engineering could go further.

I’m recruiting a senior frontend engineer for a San Francisco-based, venture-backed retailer for petite women. They’ve launched 10+ private-label lines off feedback from 100,000+ customers, and are backed by Tribe Capital, True Global, and Y Combinator…

Hi Bryan,

I’m Lucas. Your About page says to email and introduce myself, so here I am. I made Play Higher Lower, and seeing 1D Chess in your Links #15 post made me think it might suit a future one.

You see two things and guess whether the second number is higher or lower. There are 85 games, on things like Spotify streams, box office totals, mountain heights and NBA salaries, plus a Daily Challenge where everyone gets the same comparisons. It’s free, runs in the browser and needs no account: https://playhigherlower.com/

It’s a hobby project and doesn’t make money. If it isn’t a fit, no need to reply, and I won’t follow up. If you’d rather not hear from me again, just reply “no thanks”…

hey Bryan,

someone sent me bryanbraun.com and I had to reach out because music box is such a joyful little thing to exist.

I have a question - what made you keep polishing music box instead of moving on?

If I look closely, I can still discern between the real people and the marketing campaigns, but it takes effort (for that last one, I needed to look up the person’s socials). It’s not long, I fear, until it’ll be impossible to tell the difference.

And what if I’m the one trying to send the personal email. How am I supposed to signal that I’m a real person? Is there anything I could do to stand out that an LLM couldn’t operationalize? Is the cold email dead?

Maybe this will push the industry more toward in-person networking at events and conferences. I could see that being a good thing, but it could also discourage remote work, which I think would have a lot of negative consequences for workers and families.

Or maybe we all retreat to sending personal letters through the physical mail, like 19th-century academics. I admit, the thought of dropping hyperlinks into my handwritten letters makes me smile a bit.
