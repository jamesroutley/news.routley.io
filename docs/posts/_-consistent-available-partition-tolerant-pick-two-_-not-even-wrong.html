_"consistent, available, partition-tolerant: pick two"_ — not even wrong

You’re choosing a car: ferrari or jeep?

There’s plenty to consider: speed, efficiency, how good it’ll look on your driveway, etc. But you’re preoccupied by two things: Would you make your commute in under an hour? Would you spill your coffee on the way?

You’ve drawn up a table:

potholes no potholes

ferrari

<1hr, spill

<1hr, no spill

jeep

>1hr, no spill

<1hr, no spill

Either car gives sub-hour, spill-free commutes when no potholes. But when there are potholes your commutes can be sub-hour or spill-free but not both. Tradeoff!

You choose a car.

Of your car, it makes sense to ask

  1. Is it pothole-tolerant, time-wise? (ferrari yes, jeep no)

  2. Is it pothole-tolerant, spill-wise? (ferrari no, jeep yes)

but not

  1. Is it sub-hour?

  2. Is it spill-free?

Of any commutes, it makes sense to ask

  1. Were they sub-hour?

  2. Were they spill-free?

but not

  1. Were they pothole-tolerant, time-wise?

  2. Were they pothole-tolerant, spill-wise?

And it never makes sense to ask of anything simply whether it’s pothole-tolerant.

"Sub-hour, spill-free, pothole-tolerant: pick two" — not even wrong.
