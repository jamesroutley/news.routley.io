Leftover Turkey Pot Pie

Hello, 

It’s the day before Thanksgiving and I trust you’re all busy making turkey stock, tearing bread for stuffing, seasoning your turkey and debating whether or not you’ll actually make that macaroni and cheese (you will!). I’ll leave you with a few bits of wisdom for tomorrow: 

  1. You’re probably planning on making too many things. If you don’t get to one of them, nobody will know and you might be less stressed out. 
  2. If your gravy is too salty (inedibly so), the only thing to do is make a second batch without salt and combine it with the one that’s too salty. If your gravy is lumpy, add a bit of broth and strain it. If your gravy doesn’t taste like much, add some soy sauce and/or Worcestershire, maybe a splash of sherry vinegar or apple cider vinegar.
  3. Make stock with the turkey carcass. Ideally, you should get it going after you carve the bird but before you sit down—that way it will be ready to put away before you want to go to bed. This could be just to have around (turkey stock is delicious in any number of ways, even if just to sip), or to make the soup below, which I highly recommend.
  4. Send people home with some leftovers. You won’t miss them. 
  5. Remember: You like this. You like cooking, you like spending time with people, you like a full home and a large table. You like the ritual, you like caring for others and, above all, you DO like turkey, even though people are always trying to besmirch its good name. Turkey is good, and you like it!

Leftover Turkey and Greens Pot Pie

Leftover Turkey and Greens Pot Pie
Leftover Turkey and Greens Pot Pie.pdf
492 KB
Serves 4 to 6

The below recipe is flexible, but the general idea is to take any Thanksgiving leftovers you might have, hold them together with some gravy and bake it all beneath a gorgeous crust (leftover pie crust or puff pastry both work). The only ingredient you might not already have on hand is a bunch of greens, but they really do prevent this dish of leftovers from feeling like a “slop bowl.” Plus, I find eating a whole bunch of kale in and around Thanksgiving to be something I really need—even if those greens are baked in gravy beneath an all-butter crust. 

Ingredients

  • 1 bunch kale or Swiss chard
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • ½ medium onion, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced 
  • Kosher salt, freshly ground black pepper 
  • 2 cups leftover vegetables such as green beans, squash or brussel sprouts, chopped
  • 2–3 cups gravy 
  • Turkey stock, if needed 
  • ¼ cup finely chopped chives
  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped tarragon (optional)
  • 2 cups shredded or chopped turkey meat 
  • 1 10-ounce frozen puff pastry (gluten-free or regular), thawed, or 1 disc pie crust 
  • 1 large egg, beaten 
  • Thyme leaves, if you have them 

Preparation

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F. Line a sheet pan with parchment paper.
  2. Separate the stems and leaves from the kale (I use my hands). Thinly slice the stems and tear or chop the leaves into bite-sized pieces. 
  3. Heat oil in an 8–10-inch ovenproof skillet (preferably cast iron) over medium-high heat. Add onion and garlic and season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until both are softened and starting to brown at the edges, 5–8 minutes. 
  4. Add kale stems and season again with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until tender and bright green. Start to add in the leaves, handful by handful, stirring to wilt between additions.
  5. Add in chopped leftover vegetables, followed by gravy and bring to a simmer (you can thin with stock or water if needed to loosen if it feels too thick for some reason). Add in turkey, chives and tarragon (if using) and season once more with salt and pepper.   
  6. Carefully unfold the puff pastry and smooth it out using a rolling pin (or your hands). For extra credit, you can roll it into a more circular shape (approximately 12inches in diameter), but don’t stress too much about it. Working quickly, place it on top of the skillet, letting some of the dough hang off the sides (this will keep it from shrinking too much). (If using pie crust, just roll it out into a 12-inch circle.)
  7. Whisk the egg with 1 teaspoon water in a small bowl and brush it onto the top of the puff pastry. Cut three 2½-inch slits about 1 inch apart. Sprinkle the top with salt and pepper, maybe a few leaves of thyme for superfluous yet delightful decor. Place the skillet on the prepared sheet pan to catch any drips as it bakes.
  8. Bake until the pastry is baked through, deeply golden brown and the filling has started to bubble out of the top, 35–40 minutes. Let cool slightly before serving.

Turkey Pozole with Tomatillo

Turkey Pozole with Tomatillo

63 KB
Serves 6 to 8

This is a very shortcut-friendly version of traditional pozole, one that doesn’t require a blender (I quite like the larger bits of tomatillo and onion floating throughout) or a ton of time (we’re using canned hominy here). If you’re planning on making this from your Thanksgiving leftovers, make sure to add the necessary ingredients to your holiday shopping list (assuming you don’t have tomatillos lying around). Otherwise, it’s exactly the sort of low-lift, nourishing, bright, brothy pot of soup you want the day after you’ve been cooking and eating all day.

Ingredients

  • ¼ cup olive oil (or turkey fat, should you have any)
  • 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped, plus more thinly sliced, for serving
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled and thinly sliced
  • A few sprigs oregano, plus some leaves for garnish 
  • Kosher salt, freshly ground black pepper 
  • 1 pound tomatillos, husks removed, quartered 
  • 1 jalapeño, thinly sliced, divided 
  • 6 cups turkey or chicken broth 
  • 2 25–29-ounce can (or four 15-ounce cans) hominy (pozole), drained and rinsed
  • 2–3 cups shredded or chopped turkey
  • 1 bunch cilantro, leaves and tender stems, finely chopped, divided
  • 2 limes, quartered, for serving
  • 1 bunch nice radishes, thinly sliced, for serving (optional) 
  • 1 avocado, thinly sliced, for serving (optional) 
  • Tortilla chips or tostadas, crumbled, for serving (optional) 

Preparation

  1. Heat oil in a large, heavy bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add chopped onions, garlic and oregano springs and season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the onions are soft and beginning to turn golden brown, 5–8 minutes. 
  2. Add tomatillos and about ¾ of the jalapeños and season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until tomatillos begin to pop, releasing their juices. Cook down, stirring frequently, until they’re nearly broken down, all nice and jammy, 8–10 minutes.  
  3. Add turkey broth, hominy, turkey and about ¾ the cilantro. Season with salt and pepper and bring to a simmer. Cook, stirring occasionally, until all the tomatillos have melted and the hominy has taken on the flavors of the broth, 30–40 minutes. 
  4. Divide among bowls and top with thinly sliced onion and remaining jalapeños, cilantro and oregano (plus thinly sliced radishes, avocado and tortilla chips, if you like).

DO AHEAD: Pozole can be made a few days ahead, refrigerated, or several weeks ahead, frozen.
