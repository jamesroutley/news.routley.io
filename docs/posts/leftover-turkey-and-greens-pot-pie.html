Leftover Turkey and Greens Pot Pie

Serves 4 to 6

The below recipe is flexible, but the general idea is to take any Thanksgiving leftovers you might have, hold them together with some gravy and bake it all beneath a gorgeous crust (leftover pie crust or puff pastry both work). The only ingredient you might not already have on hand is a bunch of greens, but they really do prevent this dish of leftovers from feeling like a “slop bowl.” Plus, I find eating a whole bunch of kale in and around Thanksgiving to be something I really need—even if those greens are baked in gravy beneath an all-butter crust.

Ingredients

  • 1 bunch kale or Swiss chard
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • ½ medium onion, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced 
  • Kosher salt, freshly ground black pepper 
  • 2 cups leftover vegetables such as green beans, squash or brussel sprouts, chopped
  • 2–3 cups gravy 
  • Turkey stock, if needed 
  • ¼ cup finely chopped chives
  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped tarragon (optional)
  • 2 cups shredded or chopped turkey meat 
  • 1 10-ounce frozen puff pastry (gluten-free or regular), thawed, or 1 disc pie crust 
  • 1 large egg, beaten 
  • Thyme leaves, if you have them

Preparation

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F. Line a sheet pan with parchment paper.
  2. Separate the stems and leaves from the kale (I use my hands). Thinly slice the stems and tear or chop the leaves into bite-sized pieces. 
  3. Heat oil in an 8–10-inch ovenproof skillet (preferably cast iron) over medium-high heat. Add onion and garlic and season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until both are softened and starting to brown at the edges, 5–8 minutes. 
  4. Add kale stems and season again with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until tender and bright green. Start to add in the leaves, handful by handful, stirring to wilt between additions.
  5. Add in chopped leftover vegetables, followed by gravy and bring to a simmer (you can thin with stock or water if needed to loosen if it feels too thick for some reason). Add in turkey, chives and tarragon (if using) and season once more with salt and pepper.   
  6. Carefully unfold the puff pastry and smooth it out using a rolling pin (or your hands). For extra credit, you can roll it into a more circular shape (approximately 12inches in diameter), but don’t stress too much about it. Working quickly, place it on top of the skillet, letting some of the dough hang off the sides (this will keep it from shrinking too much). (If using pie crust, just roll it out into a 12-inch circle.) 
  7. Whisk the egg with 1 teaspoon water in a small bowl and brush it onto the top of the puff pastry. Cut three 2½-inch slits about 1 inch apart. Sprinkle the top with salt and pepper, maybe a few leaves of thyme for superfluous yet delightful decor. Place the skillet on the prepared sheet pan to catch any drips as it bakes.
  8. Bake until the pastry is baked through, deeply golden brown and the filling has started to bubble out of the top, 35–40 minutes. Let cool slightly before serving.
