i'm not a real boy pagan, i'm a fictionalist technopagan at my best, but given the encroaching night in the northern hemisphere i've been thinking a lot about the technologies, rituals and celebrations that come with the darkness, and the ways in which humans track our passing days. i am slowly working on a technopagan "wheel of the year" inspired... thing, basically to keep up a level of awe, reverence and romanticism. here's a preview!

winter (december - february)

food

  • roasting
  • baking
  • mulling drinks
  • stewing
  • spicing

festivities

  • winter solstice
  • Xmas
  • NYE
  • Hannukah
  • Burns Night
  • Valentine’s Day

technologies

  • fire, heating and insulation
  • shelter & construction
  • fireworks (for better or worse lol)
  • electric lighting
  • astronomy

activities and practices

  • gift giving
  • planning and reviewing for the year and seasons ahead
  • appreciating warmth/heat
  • appreciating light

spring (march - may)

food

  • birthday cake (for me lol)
  • green/"spring" veggies (aspraagus! peas!
  • eggs, lamb
  • chocolate

technologies

  • agriculture - sowing, land prep
  • care (especially in birth and medicine)
  • meteorology

festivities

  • my birthday!
  • spring equinox
  • lent
  • holi
  • cherry blossom festivals
  • may day

practices and activities

  • wishes/hopes/dreams for this year of life
  • looking at new growth
  • garden tending
  • celebrating the slow return of the sun

summer (june - august)

food

  • iced food!
  • cold fruit
  • picky bits (sorry)
  • salad :)

technologies

  • water conservation and distribution
  • refrigeration and cooling

festivities

  • summer solstice
  • music festivals

practices and activities

  • going to parks/water/”outside”
  • appreciating the cold/the cool

autumn (september - november)

food

  • spicing
  • pumpkin everything!
  • pickling
  • canning
  • salting
  • brining

festivities

  • halloween
  • samhain
  • all hallows
  • burns night
  • guy fawkes night

technologies

  • agriculture - reaping, harvesting
  • fire
  • domesticity and domesticating
  • education

practices and activities

  • bonfires
  • walking on crunchy leaves
  • candles
  • academic years beginning

do you have the same seasons as these? if so, how do you celebrate them? if you have different seasons, or a lack of distinct ones, how do you celebrate the different parts of the year with different landmarks?

alternatively, do you have suggestions for the above? i struggle with summer as the sun is the enemy of my eyes and comfort, so especially looking for more for that season!
