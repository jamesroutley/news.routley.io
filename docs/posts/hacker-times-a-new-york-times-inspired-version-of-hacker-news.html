Researchers Report Direct Edits to Neural-Network Weights

An MIT CSAIL team reports early experiments in which GPT-6 Astra interpreted and modified neural-network weights without writing a training loop. In one Qwen test, the authors say a small weight change sharply improved math and instruction-following scores; the findings are preliminary.

Andrej Karpathy Builds a Video Library From Hacker News Recommendations

HackerTube gathers 6,454 YouTube videos mentioned by Hacker News readers across nearly two decades. Karpathy describes using AI coding tools to build an alternative browsing experience, with its own recommendations and no user accounts.

PostgreSQL Developer Links Release Delay to AI-Found Bugs

Robert Haas attributes the delayed release to a growing backlog of AI-assisted bug reports. He describes late feature reversions and the human review still required to turn suggested fixes into reliable code.

Readers Search for a Television That Respects Their Privacy

A Hacker News user asks for a large television that can serve mainly as a display for an Apple TV, without collecting unnecessary data. The thread invites readers to compare their experiences rather than offering a verified product recommendation.

An Archivist’s Plea: Save the Websites You Love

After a longtime fan site vanished, a web archivist makes the case for personal backups. The post walks through capture and replay tools, with a disclosure that the author works for the company behind them.

An Earlier Report Questions Ubuntu’s Kernel-Patch Delay

In an October 31 article, freenode news reported that Ubuntu’s 22.04, 24.04 and 26.04 LTS releases still lacked kernels patched for CVE-2026-98432. This is the report’s account, not a fresh verification of Canonical’s current patch status.

An Argument for Mathematics Beyond Individual Credit

In a guest essay on Terence Tao’s blog, Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue argue that mathematical work should be valued for the understanding it brings, not for outperforming other people or machines. They urge a less ego-centered response to AI’s role in research.

Central Warsaw, Rebuilt One Voxel at a Time

The project presents a three-dimensional model of central Warsaw assembled from OpenStreetMap, LiDAR and the city’s tree database, with a voxel resolution of one meter.

Vim Practice, One Editing Challenge at a Time

hjkl offers short browser-based exercises in Vim motions, text objects, search and other editing commands. Each skill progresses from guided edits to independent challenges. The site is free and requires no account, but it calls for a desktop browser and physical keyboard.

A Backstage Glimpse of the Windows 2000 Launch

In a Microsoft blog recollection, the author describes slipping into an early rehearsal and learning that Patrick Stewart would host the Windows 2000 launch. A headset-wearing crew member stood in for the actor during the run-through.
