24 Oct 2026, 8:45 a.m.

2026 NYC General Election: Judicial Candidates

In this post, I cover all eleven competitive races across the city this election (more candidates than seats), with 41 candidates total. I think all of these are also in the Times-Union’s list of races; they also, handily, link to each candidate’s website and New York State Board of Elections campaign finance info. There are also 15 non-competitive races, with 25 candidates total. I mention but did not research these, but Soft Power Vote makes recommendations for every single race in New York City, which is a staggering task that they've done in every election since 2020. They post to Instagram (viewable through Imginn proxy) and Substack, and they'll publish today, I believe. [Edited Oct. 24 afternoon to add:] The Soft Power Vote guide is up! Short version on Instagram (viewable through Imginn proxy); detailed version up as a Google Sheet.

This isn’t as thorough as I’d like for a few reasons. I had a minor injury this week; I was waiting for the Bar Associations to publish their evaluations to help me make choices in some races, and some of the Bar Associations still haven’t released them; and I wound up on a bit of a wild goose chase around candidates’ financial disclosures, as I’ll explain.

So: this is going to be messier, more bullet-point-y and less prose-y, and more of a living document than usual. If you need to vote this weekend (Saturday or Sunday, October 24 or 25), here’s what I have. After I hit Post, I'll fix up some formatting to make it easier to navigate. Monday, the LGBT bar association will publish its evaluations and I’ll add those. I don’t know whether Brooklyn Bar Association or Queens County Bar Association are doing evaluations at all this cycle; if so, I’ll add those when I get them.

And between now and Election Day, I may poke some more at candidates’ past political donations (searching past campaign contributions at the federal, state, and city levels), skim more of Scrutinize.org which provides analysis of sitting judges' records, get friends to read and summarize more paywalled articles I currently can’t read in the Brooklyn Eagle and the New York Law Journal, and request and read financial disclosures I don’t have yet. Please feel free to do some digging, especially along those lines, and leave info in the comments.

And: I am one volunteer and these are my personal recommendations. They aren't the endorsements of the local Indivisible chapter, or the other people who help sit at my weekly outreach table, or anyone but me. Please feel free to leave links to other good resources in the comments - or your own thoughts, including disagreement or corrections, as long as you're being productive and not just insulting or dismissive.
