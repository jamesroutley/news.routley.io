2026 NYC General Election: Judicial Candidates

The financial disclosures

The New York State Unified Court System has a rule that "Judicial Candidates for elective positions must file a Financial Disclosure Statement for Judicial Candidates within 20 days of becoming a candidate" with NYS UCS’s Ethics Commission, covering the previous calendar year. And those are available for public inspection upon request; the Ethics Commission redacts a little private info before providing them.

So, last week, I emailed and requested the disclosure forms for all 41 of those candidates in competitive races. Then, this week, the Commission only gave me statements for 12 of them, saying of the other 29, “The Ethics Commission does not have financial disclosure statements for the following judicial candidates you requested”.

But that doesn’t mean they didn’t file!

UCS employees, such as judges, have to file a yearly financial disclosure whether or not they’re running for anything. And UCS chases them down for it if they don’t file in time, with a “Notice of Delinquency” and eventually putting their name on a list of delinquent filers. (In contrast, the Ethics Commission isn’t hooked up to the county and state Boards of Elections, so they don’t have a list of candidates who are on the hook to file a financial disclosure, so the Ethics Commission can’t send them reminders.) If a judicial candidate already works for UCS and filed through that system, they don't have to file a fresh disclosure as a candidate: "compliance ... shall not be necessary where a judge or non-judge already is or was required to file a financial disclosure statement for the preceding calendar year pursuant to Part 40..."

It took me way too long (including multiple conversations with the Ethics Commission, and looking at patterns in the PDFs I’d been given) to figure out that the Ethics Commission had only looked people’s names up in one of their systems and not the other. Judicial candidates have to send in the statement on paper; UCS employees file it using an electronic system. The Commission hadn’t sent me any of the financial disclosures that candidates who are judges, law clerks, etc. had filled out as court employees. On Thursday, I verified this by spot-checking one of the 29: I filled out a new form and requested the financial disclosure statement that Lola Waterman, a judge in Kings County Family Court, had filed. And indeed, the Ethics Commission sent it to me straightaway.

So, my current assumption is: if someone on that list of 29 works for UCS, they almost certainly have filed the disclosure. But if they don’t work for the courts, then they never filed it, which makes them a scofflaw. And for me that counts against someone’s candidacy.
