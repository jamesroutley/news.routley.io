Phases are awesome

Published on Wed Nov 25, 2026

I love baking bagels (once per year when I’m going through a phase of wanting to open a bakery).

When I was a teenager some friends always used this line whenever we felt like we needed to justify something we were into:

IT’S NOT A PHASE MOM THIS IS WHO I AM!!

It was a joke and all, but thinking about it twenty-something years later, I’m realizing that there was some truth behind it: there’s some cultural baggage around going through phases. Think about the reputation of people who sign up for gyms after New Years’, only to drop off a month or two into the year. There can be a subtle cultural stigma around this: seeing those who do it as “quitters” or “never really serious about going to the gym in the first place.”

As someone who goes through a lot of phases of intense interest in something, I am realizing how stupid this taboo is. Phases rock!

Each time I go through a phase I come out a little different on the other side. Even if I just skim the surface of the topic or activity, some of it takes and becomes a part of me that influences other parts of my life. Or fizzles out and comes back later on, allowing me to pick up where I left off and get a little deeper. Or at the very least, comes in handy as a conversation piece when I meet someone who is also interested in that thing.

I have a zillion examples of phases that came and went which imparted something onto me nonetheless:

  • Sailing has opened my eyes to the beauty and complexity of the waterways around New York. Tides are absolutely nuts!!
  • Waves of grinding towards learning Spanish and German bent my brain far enough that I was able to realize that I am actually kind of a linguistics nerd. I’ve since toyed with Shavian and Toki Pona, both of which have made me even more fascinated by human language and writing.
  • Playing with a few Meshtastic devices helped me realize that radio is actually really complicated and it’s insane that cell phone networks work so reliably.
  • My interest in Clojure constantly waxes and wanes, but each cycle I learn a little bit more about functional programming that makes me a better programmer in imperative languages.
  • My cycles of learning how to play harmonica was the thing that finally motivated me to learn music theory. I spent at least a decade kicking the can down the road of learning what a chord was until this moment!
  • Automating stuff in my house with Home Assistant is a wormhole for precious free time. I have purchased too much and learned little in the process.

… Okay that last one is half-serious: phases aren’t always awesome. Especially the ones that just encourage you to buy a bunch of crap you don’t need. I try to watch out for these phases and nip them in the bud before they cause too much damage. Sailing was by far the most dangerous encounter: buying a sailboat is an utterly terrible idea unless you’re really cool with it occupying most of your life and money.

I’m sure the audience of this blog is not one that really needs to hear this, but I’ll say it nonetheless: phases are awesome. Lean into them, pull on the string as long as the inspiration lasts, and don’t be afraid to drop it if your interest runs dry. One thing that has served me well: take notes! You never know if you’re going to pick that thing up again and, if you do, having some notes about what you learned and what got you to stop will be invaluable for the next cycle.
