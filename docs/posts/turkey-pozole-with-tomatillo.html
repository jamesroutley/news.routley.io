Turkey Pozole with Tomatillo

Serves 6 to 8

This is a very shortcut-friendly version of traditional pozole, one that doesn’t require a blender (I quite like the larger bits of tomatillo and onion floating throughout) or a ton of time (we’re using canned hominy here). If you’re planning on making this from your Thanksgiving leftovers, make sure to add the necessary ingredients to your holiday shopping list (assuming you don’t have tomatillos lying around). Otherwise, it’s exactly the sort of low-lift, nourishing, bright, brothy pot of soup you want the day after you’ve been cooking and eating all day.

Ingredients

  • ¼ cup olive oil (or turkey fat, should you have any)
  • 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped, plus more thinly sliced, for serving
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled and thinly sliced
  • A few sprigs oregano, plus some leaves for garnish 
  • Kosher salt, freshly ground black pepper 
  • 1 pound tomatillos, husks removed, quartered 
  • 1 jalapeño, thinly sliced, divided 
  • 6 cups turkey or chicken broth 
  • 2 25–29-ounce can (or four 15-ounce cans) hominy (pozole), drained and rinsed
  • 2–3 cups shredded or chopped turkey
  • 1 bunch cilantro, leaves and tender stems, finely chopped, divided
  • 2 limes, quartered, for serving
  • 1 bunch nice radishes, thinly sliced, for serving (optional) 
  • 1 avocado, thinly sliced, for serving (optional) 
  • Tortilla chips or tostadas, crumbled, for serving (optional)

Preparation

  1. Heat oil in a large, heavy bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add chopped onions, garlic and oregano springs and season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the onions are soft and beginning to turn golden brown, 5–8 minutes. 
  2. Add tomatillos and about ¾ of the jalapeños season with salt and pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until tomatillos begin to pop, releasing their juices. Cook down, stirring frequently, until they’re nearly broken down, all nice and jammy, 8–10 minutes.  
  3. Add turkey broth, hominy, turkey and about ¾ the cilantro. Season with salt and pepper and bring to a simmer. Cook, stirring occasionally, until all the tomatillos have melted and the hominy has taken on the flavors of the broth, 30–40 minutes. 
  4. Divide among bowls and top with thinly sliced onion and remaining jalapenos, cilantro and oregano (plus thinly sliced radishes, avocado and tortilla chips, if you like).

DO AHEAD: Pozole can be made a few days ahead, refrigerated, or several weeks ahead, frozen.
