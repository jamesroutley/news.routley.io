We should all be Luddites

Aline Normoyle

The branding of Luddism as anti-technology and anti-progress is a masterstroke of propaganda. The Luddites fought against the imposition of cruel factory work that degraded both their craft and the quality of their lives. To be a Luddite is to advocate for technologies that support your freedom, autonomy, and ability to meaningfully live with others. During their time, the Luddites were an extremely popular political and technological movement. The issues they raised in 1811 have never been meaningfully resolved: rather than reach a compromise between craftsmen and factory workers, the government used state violence to kill Luddites activists and protect factory property. To this day, decisions about how technology is used in work is decided by the asset class, rather than reached democratically, and with AI, which frankly is a brittle and untrustworthy technology, people are fed up. In the US, New Deal policies did lead to a compromise between capital and wage workers: maybe the work was boring but it was stable and allowed people some leisure time to pursue activities that were meaningful to them. However, the current neoliberal economy has destroyed this compromise. It is time to renegotiate the role of technology in our lives. We have a lot to learn from the Luddite movement.

In fact, the Luddites first organized through official channels, appealing to the House of Lords, petitioning for existing statutory protections to be enforced as well as advocating for new regulations. The original factory owners were breaking the law. They hired desperate people such as orphans, widows, and those made homeless by the enclosures. Eventually, the owners were able to change the laws in their favor or when they broke the law, the state mostly looked the other way. As Dan McQuillan writes in Resisting AI, the Luddite movement grew during a historical moment with many similarities to our own. Like now, their economy was in crisis due to the Napoleonic wars. Their society was highly unequal: rulers and politicians threw lavish parties while the lower classes were starving. New machinery further threatened the dignity and sovereignty of work for the lower classes. As McQuillan notes “the top-down imposition of new machinery of production led to increased precarity at a time of already existing economic crisis”. We see the same pattern today when AI companies ignore the copyrights on their training data or when Boeing builds faulty planes that are okay’d by the FAA.

The Luddites were skilled machinists and sophisticated craftspeople. They worked within recognized trades that were regulated according to charters, guilds, and common custom. They lived during a time of “constrained capitalism” or “moral economy” where modern practices such as usury and surge pricing were considered both immoral and illegitimate. For example, during food riots in early 1811, looters did not steal food but instead took food and left money which to them constituted a fair price. Only when these methods failed, food prices spiked, and their trade continued to be undermined did the Luddites start directly confronting factory owners and politicians and then famously, breaking machines.

In his book, Blood in the Machine, Brian Merchant quotes the historian, Frank Peel, who summarized the mood of elites during this time. “those weavers were too ignorant to understand that if they were made miserable and starving, their masters were waging a great and glorious war; they knew only that their children were crying pitifully for break.. Had they been better instructed, they would have known that it was their duty to lie down and die.” In other words, the lower classes should have had the good taste to sacrifice themselves for the sake of progress. And what is progress? It is whatever those with assets, elites, decide it is.

In our current age, our elites are billionaires and elite technocrats and politicians. The entire living world is expendable. Our elites don’t just ignore climate change, poverty, mass extinctions, and disease caused by our current industrialization, but plan to double down on it while building walled bunkers for themselves.
