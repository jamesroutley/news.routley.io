Observer movies and decider movies

Warning, spoilers ahead for the movie Wildwood.

Directors, like people in general, are either observers or deciders. An observer is a person (see MBTI and AI) whose lead function is Sensing or Intuition. A decider is a person whose lead function is Thinking or Feeling. The upshot is that people's last function (the opposite of their first) triggers them. Their middle functions don't. So an observer movie is one where characters go from MacGuffin to MacGuffin like a lumbering cyclops, and a decider movie is a histrionic drama of going from person to person, from The Totally Important Wizard to Your Long Lost Mom. Deciders have issues with self identity vs their relationship with the tribe (other people). Observers have issues with their relationship with the world, overindexing on one sense or one intuition.

Often people are better at middle functions because it's easier to switch between them instead of distorting one function to act like its opposite. A thinker may find "just" using feelings awful, but switching from the thing you saw to what it meant and back is so easy you don't even notice how often the switching happens, and how you can braid them together in a way that going from first to last (thinking to feeling, say) triggers your consciousness and not in a pleasant way. Observers are like this, but they are triggered by the world, not by themselves or others. In tech speak, science/eng guys are deciders and sales/business guys are observers.

Next time you watch a movie, look at the points where the plot transitions and ask: is this a thing or a person?

Marvel movies are observer movies. Sometimes it's a place, but usually it's a physical object that isn't alive. Infinity War is about collecting magic rocks and staffs and whatever the hell. Winter Soldier is about finding a bunker (Dr Zola's) and even though Zola is a person, he's been turned into a thing, an AI.

In a decider movie the plot goes from person to person. I just saw Wildwood, which is what made this click. It turns on a baby (not the crows, she only cares cause they stole her brother), the anthropomorphic coyotes, the Dowager Governess, meeting the talking pig and pretending to be "Lord Walter", Septima, Septima dying the talking owl and eagle, the Governor, his Grima Wormtongue of an advisor, the Governess's son (above all). When Prue does travel somewhere like Rue Thurmond's place, the place matters because of who she will meet there. It is never for the sake of the place itself.

Both Mac and her son are the biggest living MacGuffins in the movie, since you never really get anything out of them except their purpose, to exist and be loved and cherished and feared for, by protagonists and antagonists. But they're infinitesimal generators of the plot, since everything moves around them, and routes through them at some point.

Whatever visibly drives the plot is the part the movie stresses about the least.

Take the Infinity Stones. How do they work? What about the vibranium shield? The serum that turns Steve Rogers into Captain America? The Tesseract? The Black Panther suit and the herb, Thor's hammer.

Who cares. Swap them for seven magic swords and the movie is the same. They're a MacGuffin. What the movie has actually worked out is the people and that's why they have so much banter. People hold their own identity and relationships with others less heavily. They lighten up. But the magic rock is Serious Business.

Decider movies flip it. The hero gets to the next person, and they're a living MacGuffin. Often if they weren't there, you could figure out what's going on (like how the Dowager Governess is a psycho) from overwhelming random bits of evidence. Ask: if they had never talked to this guy, would it have mattered? Often the answer is no, not really. Any other guy would have done. But the way these movies handle the physical world, the objects and places and how they get used, tends to be much more sophisticated. The Dowager Governess has a complicated plan involving using magic ivy to rip up a forest to excavate her son's corpse so she can resurrect him. Quite the sophisticated understanding of her physical world, albeit one with magic. But even magic has rules, and she sure intends to exploit them more than an eruv.

The Marvel guys get a rock and have no real idea what to do with it, even though they talk about it a lot. Dowager Governess's plan is run through in passing, but she obsesses over her dead son for AGES.

A single observer (so a double decider) is good at managing relationships with themselves and the tribe, and over-indexes on their one sense or one intuition. That's the cyclops. With one eye on the world, the most obvious thing in view is a Thing.

A single decider (a double observer) is the reverse. They have one crude lever for people, which is "go talk to the next guy" or "it's me vs the world".

It's kinda jarring to see because movies seem to really stick to one theme, and I often wonder if the director notices. Also movies where they seem to cut against the personality type of the director and if they could manage/control their crew.

Where is this stuff from

Two randos on YouTube who call their setup Objective Personality. I find their theories interesting and I do think there's something real about the concepts of double observers and double deciders. I believe the 4 functions of thinking/feeling/sensing/intuition are real, that people have builtin preferences between functions, and switching from first to last is inherently not easy.
