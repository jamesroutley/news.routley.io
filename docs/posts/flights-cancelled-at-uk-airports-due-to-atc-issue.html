  1. Nats is fit for purpose, says air traffic control bosspublished at 22:18 BST

    22:18 BST

    Katy Austin

    Martin Rolfe, the chief executive of Nats, tells the BBC that his organisation's systems are fit for purpose, and has apologised for Tuesday's issue.

    Speaking to the BBC, he says that while the system was now operating normally, “of course, there's a huge backlog” of flights and passengers.

    When pushed on how long systems weren’t working properly, Rolfe wasn’t sure but agrees it was “probably” in the ballpark of six hours.

    Rolfe says the focus is "about recovery" and they are "having a full investigation" into what went wrong.

    Asked if an incident like this showed Nats’ systems aren't fit for purpose, he responds: “I don't think so, no."

    He says: “Whilst we never want these things to happen - and I absolutely apologise for the fact it has happened today - I do think we're fit for purpose."

    Asked if he is considering his position, Rolfe says: "I’m obviously focusing on making sure that we get this solved.

    "My focus today has been making sure the team's in the right place, we have the right resources, the technical issues are being investigated, and the recovery happens.

    "There'll be lots of time for that later, but that's not now,” he adds.

  11. Hundreds of flights cancelled due to technical issue - what we know, what we don'tpublished at 20:21 BST

    20:21 BST

    Jack Grey

    Hundreds of flights at airports across the UK have been cancelled due to an air traffic control issue.

    Here's what we know:

    • The issue has now been fixed, according to air traffic control provider Nats, who said its systems were "starting to recover", but later added that the recovery was taking "longer than hoped"
    • The BBC understands small numbers of aircraft are being released as “trials” at various airports, with a view to ramping up to more regular flights
    • Nats said the disruption was caused by an issue with its “flight processing system"
    • The fault was widespread across the UK, and beyond, with airports in Scotland and Ireland also reporting disruption
    • Europe's busiest airline, Ryanair, earlier reported that more than 65,000 passengers had been delayed by up to eight hours

    And what we don't know:

    • The exact cause of the fault that led to the disruption, other than it being a technical issue in Nats' systems
    • How long it will take for flights to fully resume and for the backlog to be fully cleared

  12. Wizz Air brands air traffic control provider Nats 'not fit for purpose'published at 20:13 BST

    20:13 BST

    Wizz Air has branded air traffic control provider Nats "simply not fit for purpose in its current form" after a technical issue caused hundreds of cancelled flights.

    In a statement the airline says it is "extremely disappointed" that "another Nats technical failure" has caused widespread disruption.

    "A critical national infrastructure provider should not repeatedly bring the aviation system to a standstill," it says.

    It says "urgent reform is needed" and that airlines, passengers and airports "cannot continue to bear the consequences of recurring system failures".

    "We sincerely apologise to affected passengers, but today’s disruption originates from the UK air traffic control system and is entirely outside Wizz Air’s control," the statement concludes.

    Nats declined to comment on Wizz Air's statement when contacted by the BBC.
