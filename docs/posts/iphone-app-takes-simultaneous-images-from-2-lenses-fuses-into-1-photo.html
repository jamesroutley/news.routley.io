How it works

Simply press the shutter.

One press of the shutter captures two of your iPhone’s lenses together. The app then combines them into a single high-res photo, keeping the best detail possible for each part of the photo, using only real optical detail captured by each lens, with no generative AI.

Line up the shot

Frame your subject inside the brackets that indicate the more detailed lens.

Press the shutter

Photosynthesis captures from multiple lenses simultaneously, allowing for a combined capture even during motion.

Marvel at the results

Alignment and colour are matched automatically between lenses, so you get the wide framing with the inner lens’s real optical detail.

In the app

A camera and custom editor built around two-lens photos

Easy-to-understand framing

The brackets over the viewfinder indicate the area covered by the more detailed lens. Frame your subject inside them to capture the best detail.

The best of both lenses

The app processes both images, matching their alignment and colour, to deliver a single photo combining the larger area of the outer lens with the real optical detail of the inner one.

A fully-functional editor

Apply filters, adjust the image manually, crop and rotate as standard, with an extra layer of editing tools designed for two-lens photos. You can tweak the alignment of the inner lens, and ‘paint’ over any errors to correct ghosting or doubled subjects.

Frequently asked questions

Which iPhone models are supported?

Every iPhone with 2 or 3 lenses on the back from the iPhone 11 onwards is officially supported. iPhones with 2 lenses can combine both of those lenses into one image, while iPhones with 3 lenses can choose which pair they want to combine, or combine all three (currently in beta).

Is the detail created by AI?

There is no generative AI use in Photosynthesis. We do use machine learning to calculate the best alignment and colour match between the lens captures, but every pixel of detail comes from a photo taken by your camera.

Why can’t I zoom in the viewfinder?

Photosynthesis is designed to capture the most detail possible for every photo, without compromising with digital zoom. Keeping the image ‘zoomed out’ helps to frame your subject correctly for the full set of lenses. You can then focus in by cropping the photo once it has been processed.

Can I set focus manually?

The app defaults to focus in the centre of the viewfinder to optimise for the alignment of both lenses. A manual focus control would fight against this alignment.

Does it work up close?

Because the lenses are physically separate on your phone, the closer you are to the subject, the harder the two photos are to align. If they don’t align properly, you can always export the image from each lens on its own.

Where are my photos saved?

Every photo is kept inside the app so nothing is lost while the app is still installed. You can optionally backup to iCloud and/or a folder of your choosing, and you can auto-export every photo to the Photos app, as well as manually export in a number of different formats.
