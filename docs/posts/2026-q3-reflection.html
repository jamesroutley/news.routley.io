AI is the present and future of knowledge work.

— Hamilton Greene 🐷🦀 (@SIRHAMY) September 30, 2026

The world of knowledge work is changing. The advent of a new age of computing is here. It starts with AI.

Like it or not we've been bottlenecked on how we use computers for some time:

  • Typing is the main form of communication
  • Scripting / automation relegated to skilled programmers
  • Most apps highly proprietary and takes month / years to change

AI changes a lot of this calculus:

  • AIs can drive your computer
  • You can speak to them instead of typing
  • AIs can now build you custom software in minutes - soon seconds and proactively. This leads to malleable software and a whole new era of personal computing.

This change will bring with it a lot of economic changes - good and bad. Less time focused on toil but also a lot of job shifts as whole functions are wiped out by AI.

Personally I'm hopeful and excited ab the future. We're seeing an explosion of creativity and software from ppl historically unable to build it due to resources or skill. I think this is a net good. But there will be troubled times ahead.

I'm personally trying to get ahead of some of it by leaning in and seeing what the new meta is going to be like:

  • No manual coding
  • Greater focus on the outcome and product we're trying to achieve
  • Let AI build the code through software factories

It's impossible to predict the future but I'm pretty confident the way we'll be computing is going to change massively in the coming years - similar to how the internet, google search, and mobile phones changed everythign the past few decades.

I think I have a lot of FOMO from my chronically-online induced AI psychosis. Some people are making out like bandits with vibe coded apps and I have a front seat view to seeing how much AI is changing how we build software.

And yet still I find it hard to come up with and execute on ideas that people actually want to use and buy. This is clearly not a strength of mine and is one of the reasons I decided to quit being an entrepreneur a few years ago. But still it's hard to shake the feeling that I have the skills and tools at my disposal, just have to find the right idea and execute on it.

I think I feel particularly pressured to do this now because the software industry is getting shaken on all sides. We had a really good run there the past decade or two of very high paid software engineers. But as AI gets better at coding, it's likely that moat erodes. There will always be a high market for very skilled engineers and I like to think I'm pretty good at what I do but I also think that many companies don't need the best software engineers nor do they need as many as they had before and thus the total number of employed engineers and the total salaries will likely decrease.

So for stability for myself and my family, I'm once again looking to diversify somewhat by joining in on this gold rush that is the first wave of AI software.

We'll see where it goes. Given my track record likely no where crazy. But I'll probably learn some things that will be useful in keeping me competitive in my job and fodder for more shares here on the blog.

So a little anxious here, more I want to do, but we'll have to see how it pans out.

Reflect

Health

  • Score: 7/10
  • High: Most healthy I've been for things I can control
  • Low: Most sick I've been in a long time, largely from baby daycare and partner travel
  • Seed: Seeing some good diet / exercise results

I've had a weird year and quarter with my health.

I'm absolutely the most sustainably healthy I've ever been based on my inputs. I'm eating better, I have a workout routine that I look forward to and am consistent with and that avoids injuries, I feel more flexible than I did 5 years ago.

So on the whole I should feel healthier than I ever have. And yet I don't remember a period of my life where I was sicker. I think most of this is bugs picked up by my kid at daycare and my partner while traveling. My kid has some sort of cold like once a month and my partner gets sick every other week from traveling. I've only been sick ~3 times this year which is pretty good comparatively but man these bugs knocked me out - not just sniffling but fever and sore throat for days as I try to recover. Not fun!

So generally I'm feeling good and think I'm within a few pounds of my elusive 6 pack. Getting there via diet and not just more exercise feels way more sustainable as it's not predicated on pushing much harder via running or lifting which in the past has led to injuries.

But I have to admit that I've been very sick for ~15 days this year which is very high for me. So it's not all positive.

For now I'm going to stay the course and see what happens. I hear the daycare bugs are just a thing that happens so trying to acclimate to what may be our new normal. Megna's new job (remote!) may help some here by reducing exposure to randos on planes but we'll see.

Wealth

  • Score: 8/10
  • High: Setup plans for kids future
  • Low: Things are increasingly expensive
  • Seed: Keeping a decent savings commitment rate

The stock market continues to rip, mostly bolstered by the AI bubble. I personally think the bubble will pop in the next couple years mostly because the current frontier labs are over valued based on current demand and their capex is too large to maintain and I suspect new hardware will come out in the next couple years that immediately deprecates all the currently purchsed hardware. So a bubble but one that leaves us with all the AI technology and a new wave of labs will swoop in an make the most of the tech advances without all the capex debt.

So:

  • Long-term positive
  • Near-term rocky

Add in the pressure from the new middle east war on day to day goods and the general softness of the entire market outside of AI and I think we've got a recession whenever the AI economics take a downturn.

But of course I'm just a rando on the internet so this isn't advice and I'm still dollar cost averaging into the market to hedge my bets in case I'm wrong. I'm still building up a decent savings amount just in case to hedge against possible loss of jobs due to AI and to buy in to a dip if it occurs. But my DCA is still on target for my long term investment goals in the likely case I'm wrong about timelines by a high margin.

So really not much has changed with my outlook and strategy, just moving some % allocation towards savings vs investing it immediately.

We did some financial calculations for the kid and decided on setting up a simple 529 with small investments to build towards an education future. But for the most part still investing in our current ladder as we don't want to lock that much money up into education given we're not even sure what the future of education looks like with AI and our current plans to go the public education route needing likely less investment overall. By continuing in our current ladder we get a bit more flexibility with most of the same tax efficiency and performance so better when we're not fully sure how much we'll need for education.

Connect

  • Score: 7/10

  • Low: Hard to find time to hangout with people with a kid.

The biggest news is I helped get my brother a job. This was something I wanted to do during my parental leave and while we did get started there just never got through the whole process. This time I took more of the search on myself by helping with sending in applications and coordinating interviews and we finally landed somewhere!

The job exceeded what I was looking for:

  • Full time with benefits
  • More pay than I expected
  • Stable and close workplace

So I'm overall excited and relieved.

Now a few weeks in he seems to be enjoying the role, making some friends, getting some exercise in (can see it in his arms!), and on a more reasonable adult wakeup schedule.

So big win in my book.

On the friend side we've been figuring out how to hangout with people on a kid's schedule. What's worked best for us is to have some light / flexible plans most weekend days and just invite people / try to join up with people and see if they have room. Often it's a no cause schedules are busy but by staying flexible ourselves and always having some lightweight plans made we're getting much better fill rates in the time we have available.

It is more work than I'm used to having put in and the hangouts are mostly a meal and a walk or some time at the playground but I think those are the realities of having a kid and we're adapting to them as best we can. I might also add that at this point in my life those are the kinds of lightweight hangouts I have bandwidth for - fun, fulfilling, and don't leave a hangover the next day.

So friends are hard with a kid but we're getting the hang of the scheduling part at least.

On the flip side we're spending so much time with the kid and it's very fulfilling. She's now walk/running and babbling where most sounds are words even if we don't know what they all are. It's a fun age but she's always on so we have to be too.
