hype, expectations and AI

17 Nov, 2026

last updated 1 day, 7 hours ago

everyone and their mum in the technology industry is on a spectrum: giddily waiting for the bursting of the AI bubble, or angrily dismissing the idea that the AI boom is "just" hype and a massive bubble. the economics of it aside, which really aren't my area, i don't think hype of this scale can ever match what actually happens in real life. no matter how promising a technology is, no matter how life-changing it could be, it's never going to be as good as what is sold to us to get us to sign up to rolling it out to the masses. it's always going to be over-promise and under-deliver.

maybe a caveat to that, as always seems to be the caveat people bring up at the moment, is in more of the biological sciences? a permanent cure for cancer, speculatively, or a vaccine for polio, concretely, seem to be pretty empty of downsides? at the same time, i have no doubt business interests/capitalism would find a way to make a universal vaccine for cancer a dark day for us all at this point, and that the road to a cancer vaccine would probably be paved with the blood of many as are so many things in this world. i read the immortal life of henrietta lacks like everyone else, and i know that few things come with no pain or grief for someone.

to return to hype though. i think the dominant narrative i see is that the LLM boom will be like railway mania or the dotcom boom and bust, and less like the subprime mortgage scandal, in that we have built lasting, useful infrastructure and made huge strides in technology that will lead to profit, prosperity, the new industrial revolution, AGI, the machine god, you name it, depending on who you ask.

i'm only mildly being a hater when i say that i don't understand how data centers are a similar investment to railways or subsea cables[^3]? the hardware doesn't last that long, and we have no other use for data centers more power (and, debatably, water) hungry than many cities right now?

i see the productivity gains in some areas, but i don't see how this is an industrial revolution or even a power multiplier in the same way as the looms the luddites smashed? i see the potential to disrupt people's need and right to food, water and a good life, and see most of the good of this technology concentrated in the hands of the extremely few.

but yes, hype. is it just a smokescreen? something to stop the masses from protesting peacefully outside of their nearest hundred gigawatt data center building project? is it a natural next step of human (collective) urge to build bigger and better and grow and grow, no matter the cost or likelihood of success?

how are you feeling hype wise? are you barely able to contain your AI-based glee? are you holding a wake for human craft? let me know!
