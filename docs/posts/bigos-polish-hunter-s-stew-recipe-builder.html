Bigos (Polish Hunter's Stew) Recipe Builder

No two Polish families make bigos the same way, and that is the whole character of the dish. It began as whatever the hunt and the larder produced, so the argument about what belongs in it has been running for a couple of hundred years and is not going to be settled here.

The Decisions That Actually Matter

  • Sauerkraut to fresh cabbage The biggest single decision. All sauerkraut gives you the sharp, traditional version, the one most people picture when they think of bigos. Adding fresh white cabbage softens it. My own recipe runs half and half.
  • Something smoked Smoked kielbasa, bacon or lardons are what separate bigos from an ordinary cabbage stew. If you take every smoked element out, you have made something good, but you have not really made bigos.
  • Dried mushrooms, not fresh Dried boletus or porcini are the flavour engine, and the water you soak them in becomes part of the braise. Fresh mushrooms bring water instead of concentrated flavour.
  • Time Bigos is not finished when the cooking stops. It is finished after it has been cooled and reheated a few times, which is why the builder asks how many days you have.
  • How you cook it A slow cooker cannot scorch the base, which is the commonest way bigos is ruined. The hob gives the most control and the most risk. The oven sits between the two and needs almost no attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a right ratio of sauerkraut to fresh cabbage?

No, and I will tell you so at length. A great many people use sauerkraut alone, and the historical argument is on their side: before refrigeration there was no sweet cabbage around in December. My own recipe runs half and half, which keeps the sourness clear without it dominating. Anywhere between all sauerkraut and about a third fresh is defensible.

Why does my bigos taste bitter?

Two usual causes. The first is a scorched base, which is easy to do on the hob and turns the whole pot bitter from one burnt patch. The second, and the one most people miss, is grit in the dried mushroom soaking water. Strain it through a fine sieve and leave the last splash in the jug rather than tipping it all in.

Should I rinse the sauerkraut?

Not if it is good sauerkraut. Draining is fine, but rinsing pours away the very sourness you bought it for and then replaces it with water. The exception is aggressively sour supermarket kraut, which can take a quick rinse.

Can I put bigos in jars to keep it?

No, and please do not follow the Polish recipes that describe repeat pasteurising. Bigos is a low-acid meat stew, and preserving it that way at home carries a real risk of botulism. Keep it in the fridge for four to five days, or freeze it for up to three months, which is what most Polish families do with the Christmas batch anyway.

Why is bigos better on the second day?

The sourness settles back, the rendered fat carries the smoke through everything, and the cabbage stops tasting like separate ingredients in a pot. Nothing you can do in one session gets you there. Cook it, cool it, chill it, and reheat it the next day.

Can I make bigos without a slow cooker?

Easily. The oven is the option people forget, and it is the most forgiving of the three, because the heat surrounds the pot instead of hitting it from underneath so it cannot catch. A covered casserole at 150C for about three hours does the job.

How do I pick good dried mushrooms?

Look at the slices before you buy. Small holes and tunnels running through the caps mean larvae have been through them at some point, which is completely normal in wild-picked mushrooms and not worth panicking about. What you want to avoid is a bag with a layer of fine dust or powder in the bottom, because that is what is left behind after they have eaten their way through, and anything that smells musty or of ammonia rather than of forest floor.

Buy whole caps or proper slices rather than the cheapest bag of fragments, which tends to be stems and offcuts and gives you far less flavour for the money. When you get them home, put the bag in the freezer for a couple of days before moving them to an airtight jar. That settles anything still living in there and costs you nothing.

Soak them in a bowl rather than straight in the pot, so you can see what floats out, and taste the strained liquid before it goes anywhere near the bigos. If it is clean and earthy it is the best ingredient you have. If it tastes bitter, pour it away and use water instead, because a bitter bigos cannot be rescued afterwards.
